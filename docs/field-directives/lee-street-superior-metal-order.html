Read this before you run it

Color match — the one that bites

We are bringing a physical scrap of the existing white trim. Match to the scrap, not to a color name. The existing has been on the building through a summer, so a nominal name match can still read wrong beside it — and these two whites will sit six inches apart on a red field where everyone looks up at them.

If you cannot confirm a match from the scrap, call before running. We would rather change the plan than hang the wrong white.

Context so the shop knows what this is doing: the canopy beam was field‑cut and is out of plane, so the existing splice trim never had a flat surface to land on and the reveal at the joint wanders. This channel slips up over the whole condition and hides it. Nothing here is structural — it is all appearance, which is exactly why the color match and the flatness of the exposed face are the whole job.

Questions for Superior

  1. Can you have 24 sticks Wednesday morning? If not, what is the soonest — and can we take the channel first and pick up the small items after?
  2. Does the scrap match anything you already stock? A same‑coil match beats a close match every time.
  3. Is the existing SMP or PVDF? If you can tell from the scrap, tell us — we want the same coating system, not just the same color name.
  4. At our exposed face width, do you want a crown or a light center vee in it? You form more of this than we do. If a flat face that wide will oil‑can on you, say so and put a break in it — a wavy white line overhead would be worse than what we are covering.
  5. Do you stock the painted stainless rivet in this white, or do we source it separately?
