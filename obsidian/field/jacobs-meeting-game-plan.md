## First — read this before the "free upgrade" section

You asked for a sheet proving PVC costs more so you can tell him you gave him a free upgrade.

**The data is real and it's in Appendix A. But do not lead with it, and do not use the word "upgrade."** Here's why, plainly:

1. He didn't complain about value. He complained about **not being told**, and about **seams**. Answering a trust complaint with a price argument reads as deflection, and he will hear it that way instantly.
2. "We gave you a free upgrade" claims it was a *decision*. If the QXO ticket shows a stock pull, it was a *mistake*. A sharp client will take that apart, and then the story becomes "you lied about it" — which is far worse than the original error.
3. He's paying **$133/sf**. Nobody at that price feels like they're getting anything free.
4. It invites the worst possible question: *"What else did you decide without telling me?"*

**The right use of the cost data is defensive, held in reserve.** Bring it out only if he demands a tear-off back to TPO, demands a credit for "inferior material," or someone argues he got shorted. Then it answers the question factually.

**The line, if it comes to that:**

> "The sheet that went on is a higher-spec membrane than what we sold you — thicker over scrim, higher breaking strength, more than double the seam strength. I'm not saying that to excuse not telling you. I'm saying it so you know you weren't shorted on material. What you're owed is the choice you didn't get, and a clean warranty."

That's honest, claims no credit, and shuts down a credit demand.

# Appendix A — Cost differential, PVC vs TPO

**Reserve material. See the warning at the top.**

### Published cost ranges

| | TPO | PVC |
|---|---|---|
| Membrane material only | $1.00–$2.50/sf residential; $1.75–$3.75/sf commercial by thickness (45/60/80 mil) | consistently higher than comparable TPO |
| Installed, typical | $5.50–$9.50/sf | $7.50–$12.50/sf |
| Installed, commercial | $6.50–$11.50/sf | $9.00–$14.00/sf |
| Installation labor | baseline | **15–25% higher** than comparable TPO |

### Why PVC costs more

PVC membranes are built on PVC resin plus plasticizers and additive packages for flexibility, UV resistance and color — a more expensive raw-material formulation than TPO's polypropylene/ethylene-propylene rubber blend. Installation also runs more, typically 15–25% above a comparable TPO system.

### Spec differential on these two specific products

From Flex's own data sheets — same manufacturer, same 60 mil, same reinforced sheet:

| | Sold: 60 TPO Plus | Installed: MF/R 60 PVC |
|---|---|---|
| Thickness over scrim | 0.0221" | **.030"** (+36%) |
| Breaking strength | 305 × 290 lbf | **375 × 343 lbf** |
| **Seam strength** | 135 lbf | **297 lbf** (2.2×) |
| Elongation @ break | 30% min | **91% × 97%** |
| Warranty tiers | 5/10/15/20 yr | 5/10/15/20 yr |

### ⚠️ The number that actually proves it is your own invoice

National ranges are context, not evidence. **The proof is the QXO ticket** — what the grey Flex PVC cost per roll versus what Flex 60 TPO Plus would have cost on the same order.

Pull that before you rely on any of this. If R&B's actual cost on the PVC was higher, you have a hard, checkable number. If it wasn't, **do not make the argument at all.**
