## PART 2 — Manufacturer technical rep call

Make this call before the site meeting. Reps come out same-day for warranty issues. Getting them to the site is the single highest-value thing you can do tomorrow.

**Open with:** "I have a residential terrace deck with your PVC installed over occupied space. I need a technical determination and I need it today. Can you get a rep to the site?"

Then, in order:

1. **Confirm the product.** Read them the label. Is this approved for a terrace deck over living space with a pedestal deck system on top?
2. **The seam question — ask it flat:** *"Is an adhesive-lapped seam ever an approved seaming method for this membrane, or is hot-air welding the only approved method?"* Get the answer clean. Do not lead them.
3. **If the seams aren't welded — what's the required remedy?** Can the laps be over-welded or stripped in with a cover strip, or does the field sheet have to come out? This determines whether you're doing a repair or a re-roof, and it determines the price.
4. **Warranty status.** What does it take to get the warranty issued on this deck? Does anything already installed disqualify it? Who has to inspect?
5. **Weld verification protocol.** *"What is your required field test for seam integrity, and what should an owner's inspection actually look at?"* — This is the important one. It converts Scott's "I want to inspect all welds" into a defined manufacturer-sanctioned test that everyone can agree on instead of an open-ended veto.
6. **Compatibility checks.** Is there any TPO anywhere on this job that this PVC has to tie into? (PVC and TPO cannot be welded to each other.) Any asphaltic material in contact with the PVC?
7. **Pedestal deck over the membrane.** Does the warranty permit it, and does it require a protection or slip sheet between membrane and pedestals?
8. **Close:** *"Send me the repair specification and the weld test protocol in writing today."*

## The technical point that makes Ben's plan work

Ben's instinct to keep the membrane down is **right**, and it happens to line up with the correct repair.

If the laps have adhesive between the plies, you can't clean and re-weld them — but a **heat-weldable cover strip welded over the existing lap** needs the membrane exactly where it is. Nothing gets pulled apart. Two fresh welds onto clean membrane on either side of the old lap.

So the client's preferred approach and the manufacturer-correct approach are the same approach. The only thing that changes from what Ben told Scott is *how the seam gets made* — and that's a detail you can present as the manufacturer's spec rather than a reversal.

**Still get it from Flex in writing before a board comes up.**

## Expect the pick to open the seams — and welcome it

If those laps were adhesive-bonded, Scott's pick will go through them. Expect that to happen in front of you.

Do not dread it. **Say it first:** *"Probe them — that's the right test, and it's the test we'll use on the rework."* Him proving the condition with his own tool is better for you than you asserting it. It documents the defect, sets the repair scope by evidence instead of argument, and it puts you on the same side of the test.
