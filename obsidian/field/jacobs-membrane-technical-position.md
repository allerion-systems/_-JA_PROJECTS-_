**The grey 60 mil Flex product on the shelf is PVC. The Flex TPO Plus is white.**

That is the most likely mechanism here: somebody asked for grey Flex 60 mil and got exactly what that description maps to at the counter — PVC. Same manufacturer, same gauge, same reinforced mechanically-fastened sheet, different polymer, because grey is not the stock TPO color in that line.

## 2. The defensible position — and what not to argue

**What is true and worth saying:**

- Same manufacturer, same 60 mil gauge, same polyester-reinforced mechanically-fastened construction. This is not a downgrade in product class.
- **PVC is not the cheap option.** It typically costs more than TPO, has better chemical and grease resistance, and has a far longer field track record — PVC has been in service since the 1960s; TPO was commercialized in the 1990s and had real early formulation problems. For a terrace over a kitchen, PVC is arguably the better membrane.
- Both are thermoplastics. Both are hot-air welded. The remediation path is the same either way.
- The remedy is definable, manufacturer-sanctioned, and inspectable — which is exactly what Scott asked for.

**What not to argue, at all:**

- **Do not argue that the substitution didn't matter.** He specified a system and a color and got something else without a conversation. That grievance is legitimate and arguing it will cost you everything the technical facts buy you.
- **Do not argue the $133/sf.**
- **Do not lead with "PVC is better."** It reads as excuse-making. It belongs in the remedy conversation — what he's owed — not in the apology.

The honest frame: *the material class is right and arguably better, the color and disclosure were wrong, and the seams have to be made right.*

## 3. The problem with the plan Ben gave Scott — read this before anyone pulls a board

Ben's plan as relayed: **pull deck boards → remove Instadeck pedestals → clean all seams → heat weld the seams.**

The sequence is right. **The middle step probably will not work as described.**

### The core issue

If the laps were bonded with adhesive, **the adhesive is between the two sheets.** Hot-air welding needs bare membrane against bare membrane. You cannot clean adhesive out of a lap without separating the sheets first, and pulling apart cured adhesive laps typically stretches, scars, or delaminates the sheet — leaving you worse off than you started.

**The industry-standard remedy for exactly this condition is to strip in the seam with a heat-weldable cover strip** — weld a new strip of membrane over the existing lap so you get two fresh welds onto clean membrane on either side of it. Every major manufacturer sells cover tape for this purpose, specifically for seam overlays and for membrane that won't accept a direct weld.

**If Ben starts pulling laps apart on the assumption they'll re-weld, you may turn a strip-in repair into a tear-off.**

### Three more things the plan doesn't cover

1. **Weathered membrane prep.** Membrane exposed beyond roughly 7 days needs a weathered-membrane cleaner before welding, and where cleaning isn't enough, abrasion with a Scotch-Brite pad. Not optional, and not what "clean the seams" usually means to a crew.
2. **Lap width.** Minimum hot-air weld width is **1½"** across the industry. If the crew lapped the sheets for adhesive, the lap may be too narrow to yield a compliant weld even after cleaning — another reason the answer is likely a cover strip.
3. **Pedestal reinstallation.** Confirm whether Flex requires a protection or slip sheet between the membrane and the Instadeck pedestals. Point loads on a membrane are a warranty question.

### Why this is urgent tonight

**Scott has already agreed to Ben's method over the phone.** If Flex comes back and says the seams have to be stripped in rather than re-welded, R&B has to walk back a plan the client already accepted — and a second reversal on this job is the thing you genuinely cannot afford.

**Get Flex's method in writing before any board comes up.** If the method changes, tell Scott proactively with the manufacturer's reason attached. Coming to him first with "the manufacturer's spec is actually X" reads as diligence. Getting caught changing it mid-rework reads as more of the same.

## 4. The weld QC protocol — hand this to Scott

He said he wants to inspect all welds. Give him the real industry standard, in writing, before he invents his own. This turns an open-ended veto into a defined test you can actually pass:

- **Test weld at the start of each shift** — morning and afternoon — on like material over the same substrate, and again whenever ambient conditions change materially
- **Field peel test** on each test weld before production welding begins
- **Minimum 1½" weld width** on all seams
- **Probe every seam after it has cooled** with a blunt probe. A sound weld resists the probe; a cold or false weld opens
- **Photograph and log** each probed seam by location so there's a record of what he approved
- **Manufacturer field inspection** before decking returns, if Flex will send someone

Offering this before he asks for it is the single strongest move available to you tomorrow. It says: we already know the standard, we're inviting the test.
