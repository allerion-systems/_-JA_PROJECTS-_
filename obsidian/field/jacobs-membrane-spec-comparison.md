## ⚠️ Correction to what I told you earlier

I previously inferred that grey only comes in PVC in the Flex line. **That is wrong, and do not say it.**

The Flex 60 TPO Plus data sheet states: *"Available in White, Gray or Tan."* The PVC sheet lists the same three. **Both products are made in gray.**

What remains true is only the narrower supplier-level observation: QXO's listings show Flex TPO Plus 60 mil stocked in **white** and Flex 60 mil MF/R in **grey**. That is a stocking pattern, not a manufacturer constraint. It may still explain the counter mix-up — but it is a lead to verify against the QXO ticket, not a claim to make.

## The seam question — this is now definitively answered

Flex's own sheets settle it. From the **TPO** data sheet:

> "The Flex 60 TPO Plus membranes are true thermoplastics, therefore **all seams are sealed by the hot air welding method** creating a seam as strong as the original membrane."

> "For adhered systems, the membrane is laminated to the appropriate substrate using Flex Bonding Adhesive. **All seams are completed by the hot air welding method.**"

From the **PVC** data sheet:

> "The Flex MF/R 60 PVC Roofing Membrane can be installed with Flex's Bonding Adhesive for a fully adhered installation."

> "**Hot air welded seams are the strongest, most reliable seams in the roofing industry.**"

**Read those together and the picture is clean.** Flex Bonding Adhesive is for laminating the membrane **to the substrate** — that is what "fully adhered" means in Flex's own language. In *both* product lines and in *both* attachment methods, mechanically attached and fully adhered, **the seams are hot-air welded.** There is no adhesive-seam option in either system.

So a "fully adhered" installation was a legitimate method. Using bonding adhesive **at the lap instead of welding it** was not. That is the defect, it is narrow, and it is fixable — and it is the only thing on this job that genuinely has to be made right.

### What this table actually says

**On the numbers that matter structurally, the installed PVC outperforms the TPO that was sold:**

- **Seam strength 297 lbf vs 135 lbf — more than double.** On a job where seams are the entire issue, the installed membrane has the stronger seam spec.
- **Thickness over scrim .030" vs .0221" — about 36% more.** This is the number roofers actually judge longevity by, not nominal thickness.
- **Breaking strength higher in both directions.** 375×343 vs 305×290.
- **Elongation roughly 3× higher** — better accommodation of structural movement.
- **Same warranty tiers**, 5 through 20 years, on both products.
- **Same color availability.**

**Where TPO is better:** tear strength (75×130 vs 58×82). And TPO Plus carries LEED and CRRC listings — but note the TPO sheet qualifies those to **"White and Tan"** only. On a **gray** roof, that advantage was never on the table. Gray PVC at least has published reflectivity and SRI figures; gray TPO does not appear on the TPO sheet at all.

## How to use this — and how not to

**Do not open with this.** Not tomorrow, not on the roof. Leading with "actually ours is better" on a job with an unwelded seam is how you lose a client who is currently willing to let you fix it.

**Sequence it:**

1. **Own the two real failures first.** He was not told, and the seams were not welded. No qualifiers.
2. **Fix the seams to Flex's written spec.** That is not negotiable and it is not a bargaining chip.
3. **Then**, and only if remedy or credit comes up, this table is your ground. The question becomes *what is he actually owed* — and the honest answer is a disclosed choice and a compliant warranty, not a different membrane. He was not given a lesser product.

If he asks directly what's on his roof — answer plainly, hand him both sheets, and let the numbers speak. Do not editorialize over them.

## Verify before you rely on any of it

- [ ] **Roll label photos.** Confirm Flex MF/R 60 PVC and the color designation. The QXO listings show both "Light Grey" and "Special Dark Grey" — know which one is up there.
- [ ] **The QXO ticket.** R&B account 590227, Kyle Raymer branch manager. The SKU on that ticket is what settles how this happened.
- [ ] **Current revision.** The PVC sheet I pulled is dated **08/2017** and carries the Leesport, PA address; the TPO sheet carries the Reading, PA address. **Ask Flex for the current revision of both** before handing anything to a client or an adjuster.
- [ ] **Applicator status.** The TPO sheet states the system *"must be installed by an authorized Flex Roofing Systems TPO applicator."* Confirm R&B's authorized status for PVC — it is a warranty precondition.
- [ ] **Warranty after remediation.** Ask Flex directly what tier they will issue on this deck once the seams are corrected, and what inspection they require.
- [ ] **Repair method.** Still the open question: re-weld the existing laps, or strip in with a cover strip. Get it in writing before a board comes up.
