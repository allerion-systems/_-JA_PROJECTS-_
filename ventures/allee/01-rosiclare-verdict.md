## 1. The economics are zero before you spend a dollar on capital

The decisive source is peer-reviewed and it is not from a vendor:

**Buehlmann, U. & Thomas, E. (2025), "The Impact of Cost and Price Fluctuations
on U.S. Hardwood Sawmill Profit," *BioResources* 20(3), 5587–5601** — Virginia
Tech and the USDA Forest Service, modeling 4, 8 and 12 MMBF eastern red oak
mills in LORCAT with real Kentucky Division of Forestry delivered log prices and
real Hardwood Market Report lumber prices.
([full PDF](https://ojs.bioresources.com/index.php/BRJ/article/download/24618/1944/18040))

**Their starting premise is the finding:**

> *"given the current depressed market for lumber, sawmills, on average, break
> even but make zero profits."*

**12 MMBF red oak mill, mid-2024, at exact break-even:**

| | Annual | Per MBF lumber |
|---|---:|---:|
| Product value (lumber + residues) | $8,940,440 | **$746** |
| Log cost | $4,463,806 | **$373** |
| Operating cost | $4,522,928 | **$378** |
| **Profit** | **~$0** | **$0** |

Same result at 8 MMBF. Same at 4 MMBF. **Not thin margins — zero.**

### The sensitivity is savage

| Move | Annual profit impact, 12 MMBF |
|---|---:|
| 0.1% rise in operating cost | −$4,510 |
| 0.1% rise in log cost | −$4,411 |
| 0.1% fall in lumber price | −$3,419 |
| 10% fall in lumber price | −$342,230 |
| **25% combined adverse move** | **−$3,093,668** |

And hardwood sawmill operating costs **rose 30–40% between 2020 and 2024**.

### Now put debt on it

**My capex estimate for a greenfield build is $8.4M–$22.8M** (UNVERIFIED — get
vendor quotes; the two hard datapoints are a **single McDonough hybrid edger at
~$517,000** and E&E Hardwoods' **$2.1M to *rebuild*** a Bell County KY mill on
an existing, powered, permitted site with an existing crew).

Run the model at **+25% lumber prices — which is essentially the all-time record
of June 2022**, when the HMR hardwood composite hit $1,240/MBF:

| Mill | Profit at record prices | Debt service (8%, 10 yr) | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 MMBF on a $20M build | $855,602 | ~$2.9M | **−$2.0M** |
| 8 MMBF on a $10M build | $568,847 | ~$1.46M | **−$890K** |
| 4 MMBF | $285,037 | any greenfield capex | not a business |

> 🔴 **You lose money at the top of the best hardwood market in recorded
> history.** Change any input you like — you cannot change the sign.

**Two more penalties the model does not include, both of which hit you:**

- Buehlmann & Thomas warn explicitly that *"a small sawmill most likely will pay
  higher prices for their logs compared to a large mill."* As a new, unproven
  buyer with no logger relationships, **your log cost is above the $373 they
  modeled.**
- **Residues flip from break-even to a cost at this site.** They price chips at
  $25/ton and bark/sawdust at $17/ton against a **$17/ton** trucking quote for
  50 miles — and conclude residues *"are not currently a profitable aspect of
  sawmill operation."* I could identify **no pulp, OSB or pellet consumer within
  50 miles of Rosiclare.** You would be paying to haul your waste away.

## 2. The market is in secular collapse, not a cycle

| | |
|---|---:|
| US sawn hardwood production, 2022 | 7,149 MMBF |
| 2023 | 5,545 |
| **2024** | **5,079 — −29% in two years** |
| Eastern production projected 2026 | **4.08 BBF — lowest since HMR began tracking in 1960** |
| 1998 | **"well over 12 billion board feet"** (NHLA) |

That is a **~67% collapse over 27 years.** Over the same window **softwood
production rose.** Hardwood's share of US wood production fell from 21% (2022)
to **16% (2024)**.

**Every demand pillar is down at once:**

- **Furniture is gone.** Imports went from 6%/23% of US upholstered/wood
  household furniture value in 1997 to **35%/78% by 2019.**
- **Flooring:** **engineered now holds 74%** of the hardwood flooring market —
  a thin veneer over plywood, consuming a fraction of the sawn hardwood that
  solid strip did.
- **Pallets switched species:** the industry is now ~**80% softwood**, a
  reversal of the historical norm. That was your volume floor.
- **Crossties are failing too** — a "major downturn in Class I acquisition" in
  2026 on merger uncertainty and oversized inventories. Ties have been the
  third-largest hardwood sector since 2008. **All three legs at once.**
- **China:** banned US log imports **March–November 2025**. The US went from
  China's **top supplier in 2024 to fourth in 2025**; market share **22% → 9%**;
  export value **$612M → $170M in a single year.** Raw material exports to China
  are down **59% from the 2018 peak.**

**And the mills are dying:**

- **35 hardwood sawmills closed in 2025** (NHLA); 2026 expected higher.
- The Hardwood Federation estimates **one sawmill goes out of business every
  week.**
- **~40,000 jobs lost since 2022** — 420,000 → 380,000.
- **US sawmill capacity dropped 6% YoY in Q1 2026.** Utilization ticked up to
  71.8% **only because capacity is shrinking faster than production.**

> **Utilization is rising because mills are dying, not because demand is
> recovering. You would be adding capacity into a market destroying capacity as
> fast as it can.**

**One genuine bright spot, and read what it actually says.** Kentucky Q1 2026
delivered log prices: white oak high-quality **+12.8%**, low-quality **+15.4%**,
on lumber, veneer and **stave** demand. Red oak high-quality only +3.2%, demand
"lackluster."

**The log market is stronger than the lumber market.** For a sawmill that is the
wrong direction on *both* sides of the spread simultaneously — and it is exactly
why 35 mills closed last year.

## 3. Rosiclare is close to the worst site in the eastern hardwood belt

| | |
|---|---:|
| Rosiclare population, 2020 | **980** (peak 2,086 in 1950 — **−53%**) |
| Hardin County population | 3,649 — **least populous county in Illinois** |
| **Total employed persons, entire county** | **~1,270** |
| Poverty rate | 22.3% |

Fluorspar — mined commercially since 1842, "Fluorspar Capital of the World" —
**ended in 1996.** There has been no replacement industry in thirty years.

**River access:** there is **no commercial river terminal in Rosiclare.** The
dock in City Park is a **recreational boat dock with 12 campsites**. Golconda
downriver is a 206-slip **recreational marina**. The historic barge loading was
fluorspar infrastructure that left with the mines.

The nearest operating public terminal is **Shawneetown Regional Port District**,
~35–40 road miles upriver — and note its price tag: Governor Pritzker broke
ground **28 August 2025** on a **$27 million** fertilizer terminal, **on
repurposed existing infrastructure**, creating **~50 jobs**. *That is the state's
own cost for river access.*

**Roads:** IL-1, IL-34, IL-146 — **all two-lane.** IL-34 **dead-ends in
Rosiclare at the river**, at a defunct ferry crossing. No four-lane in the
county. Nearest interstate is I-24 near Metropolis, ~40–45 miles by two-lane.

**Rail: effectively none.** On the IDOT railroad map, "HARDIN" and "POPE" appear
only as *county* labels — no rail-served point in either county. Nearest are
Eldorado/Carrier Mills (~35 mi NW) and Joppa (~45 mi SW). The county had rail in
1912; those lines died with the mines.

**Labor is the hard stop.** A 12 MMBF hardwood mill needs roughly 25–40 people
(estimate). **You would be hiring 2–3% of the county's entire working population**
into physically demanding, cyclical work. BLS mean for NAICS 321100 is $23.11/hr;
sawing machine operators $19.65/hr.

**Utilities: UNVERIFIED and this is a first-phone-call item.** No published data
on three-phase capacity or gas service at any Rosiclare site. In a town of 980
that lost its industrial base in 1996, **do not assume the distribution capacity
is still there.** City of Rosiclare, (618) 285-3445.

**And the procurement radius is worse than the map suggests.** Logs travel 60–100
miles maximum. **Roughly half of Rosiclare's circle is across an unbridged river**
— there is no bridge at Rosiclare and the ferry is defunct — **and much of the
Illinois half is the Shawnee National Forest**, which is not a commercial log
supply. Meanwhile **Independent Stave (five American stave mills) and
Brown-Forman (four sawmills, two cooperages, 750K–1M barrels/yr) will outbid you
for every good white oak log.**

**Incentives are real and irrelevant at this scale.** Hardin County *is* a
federal **Opportunity Zone** (tract 17069970900, through 31 Dec 2028). But an OZ
**defers and reduces tax on gains you already have — it does not make an
unprofitable business profitable.** On a losing operation it converts a taxable
gain into a permanent capital loss with a tax wrapper on it. Enterprise Zone
status for Hardin County is **UNVERIFIED** — SIRP&DC, (618) 252-7463.

## 4. Barge is out, and for a different reason than you'd think

The Ohio moves 184M+ tons a year, and the top commodities are **coal, crude
materials and petroleum.** Hardwood lumber is neither heavy-bulk nor low-value.

A barge holds ~1,500 tons; green 4/4 red oak is ~4,000–4,500 lb/MBF. **One barge
is 650–750 MBF — more than half a year's output of a 1.2 MMBF/month mill —
sitting uncovered, in the weather, unsold.**

The line-haul spread looks enormous (barge $0.01–0.02/ton-mile vs truck
$0.11–0.20) **and it is irrelevant**, because you must add dock amortization,
loading labor, drayage both ends, 5–10 days transit vs 1 by truck, and half a
year of working capital — and then the decisive one:

> **The receiving customer must have their own dock.** Hardwood buyers are
> furniture plants, cabinet shops, flooring plants and distribution yards.
> **They have truck docks. They do not have river docks.**

**A private dock** needs USACE Section 10 + Section 404 and IEPA 401
certification — individual permits typically **12–24 months**, plus $200K–$500K+
of engineering before a pile is driven, against the $27M Shawneetown anchor. A
mill of any plausible size at Rosiclare generates **7–17 barge loads a year.**
That is a rounding error against fixed dock cost.

**No volume you could plausibly generate justifies a dock.** If you ever needed
the river, you would buy service at Shawneetown.

## 6. What actually works, in order

### A. Re-launch the trading business — which is what it actually was

For generations the family made money as a **wholesale trading firm, not
a manufacturer.** Near-zero fixed capital, scales with working capital, and —
the part that matters now — **it gets structurally stronger in a consolidating
market.**

Thirty-five mills closed last year. The survivors lost sales departments; the
buyers lost supplier relationships. **Somebody has to move mixed grades and odd
lots between fewer sellers and a fragmented buyer base, and that somebody earns
a spread on other people's inventory risk.**

**This is the highest-return use of the Allee name and it requires no Rosiclare,
no dock, no kiln, and no bank.** It is also, precisely, what the newspaper
clipping describes.

### B. Buy distressed assets. Never build them.

Thirty-five mills closed in 2025, more in 2026, including concentration yards.
Their equipment and their **permitted, powered, rail- or highway-served sites**
are being auctioned at a fraction of replacement cost — **with existing log
supply relationships, existing crews and existing customer lists.**

> **Never pay greenfield prices for capacity in an industry retiring capacity.**

### C. Kiln-drying and concentration yard — not sawing

Buy green lumber from struggling mills who are desperate for cash and cannot
afford their own kilns. Add **$200–400/MBF** of value. Carry maybe **15–25% of a
sawmill's capital and none of its log-procurement exposure.**

Documented targets: **$25–30/MBF/day gross margin in the kiln** (UK Extension
FOR-128), degrade managed to **$15–25/MBF**, DH energy **$50–80/MBF**. Red oak
4–6 weeks, white oak 4+, hard maple up to 8 with sticker-stain grade risk,
poplar fast.

**You profit from the mills' distress instead of joining it.** Site it where the
logs, labor and highways already are — Mattoon, Harrisburg, or western Kentucky.
**Not Rosiclare.**

### D. If you must saw, be a white oak specialist

It is the one segment with a real bid (+12.8% Q1 2026), and southern Illinois is
white oak country. **But go in knowing ISC and Brown-Forman are vertically
integrated, well-capitalized, and will outbid you for every good log** — and
that cooperage demand tracks bourbon inventory cycles, which have their own
oversupply problem.

## 7. The honest summary

**Rosiclare is not the dream. The name is.**

The dream was a business that moved forty million board feet with a handful of
people and never touched it. **That business is more viable today than it was in
1955**, because consolidation has stripped the sales and sourcing function out
of an entire industry and left it lying on the ground.

**A sawmill in Hardin County is the one shape of this that cannot work.** It
takes the family's greatest asset — a name that meant *we are good for it* — and
attaches it to the highest-fixed-cost, most volume-dependent, most
capital-punishing position in a commodity that has lost two-thirds of its output
in a generation.

**Secure the name this week. It is nearly free and the clock is running. Then
put it on a business that makes money.**
