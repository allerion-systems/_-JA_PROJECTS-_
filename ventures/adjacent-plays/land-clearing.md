# Land Clearing — The Verdict

**Can Ben's skid steer generate cash while Supply gets off the ground?**
5 September 2026

> **Not in the form contemplated — and the reason has almost nothing to do with
> whether the business model works.**

---

## 1 · Step 0: the machine probably can't do the work

**Find the model number and the auxiliary GPM before reading further. Nothing
below matters until you have it.**

| | Flow | What it cuts |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard flow** | ~15–25 GPM | brush and saplings **up to about 4"** |
| **High flow** | ~30–40 GPM at 3,500–4,500 PSI | the 6–8" hardwood that actually pays |

Fecon's workhorse **BH74SS** — 61" cut, 2,790 lbs, **$40,999** — needs **30–50
GPM** and a 75–120 HP carrier. Fecon's own warning: *a mulcher starved of flow
will bog down no matter how much engine horsepower the carrier has.*

**Retrofitting high flow is reported at $20,000–$30,000** against roughly $10,000
as a factory option — pump replacement, couplers, flow selector, dealer ECU
enablement. **⚠️ That figure is forum-sourced and UNVERIFIED; the primary pages
were blocked. Get a written dealer quote.** Experienced operators' honest advice
is to sell the machine and buy one with factory high flow.

**Heads that run on standard flow:** Baumalight MS348 **$8,597** · CID disc
**$21,724** · Loftness Battle Ax L **$25,653**. All limited to brush and small
saplings — **the cheapest, most commoditised, most competed segment**, where a
homeowner with a rented machine underbids you.

> **The capital step is a cliff, not a ramp: $50,000–$110,000 to reach the
> starting line** — head, possible retrofit, rated trailer, CDL.

## 2 · The real operating cost

| Line | $/hr |
|---|---:|
| Fuel (4–5 gal/hr under load) | $14–18 |
| **Teeth** — 30–36 carbide at $80–100; **30–50 hr life in hardwood**, not the 400 hr the calculators assume | **$8–20** |
| Tracks ($2,500–4,000 / 1,200–1,600 hr) | $2–4 |
| Undercarriage ($2,000–5,000 per 1,200–1,500 hr) | $1.50–4 |
| Hydraulics at continuous max heat and load | $6–12 |
| Unscheduled repair reserve | $8–15 |
| Head depreciation ($41k / 3,000 hr) | $13.70 |
| Machine depreciation (CTL engine life in mulching: **3,300–3,900 hr**) | $20 |
| **TOTAL** | **$73–$107** |

**Kentucky is hardwood.** The optimistic tooth math you find online assumes
400-hour carbide life; real reporting is 200–500 at best, 50–100 common, and
**30–50 hours in hardwood or stump grinding.**

## 3 · What a first year actually looks like

**Ignore the $200K–$400K revenue and 40–60% net figures.** They trace to
lead-generation and marketing-services companies selling to the exact people
reading those pages. Treat every number on those sites as an advertisement.

**Utilization is the number that decides everything.** Kentucky has measurable
precipitation ~**120 days a year**, spring-peaked. Mobilisation costs **2–3
unbillable hours and $200–500 per job**. You have no reviews and no referral
base. And you are simultaneously standing up a distribution company.

**Realistic year one: 50–90 billable days. Not 200.**

```
  Revenue     60 days × $2,000                        $120,000
  Operating   360 hr × $55                            ($19,800)
  Machine depreciation reserve                         ($7,200)
  Head note   $41k over 5 yr @ 9%                     ($10,200)
  Mobilisation 60 × $200                              ($12,000)
  General liability                                    ($4,200)
  Inland marine                                        ($2,400)
  Marketing from zero                                 ($12,000)
  Licensing, tax, accounting, phone, PPE               ($4,000)
                                                      ─────────
  PRE-TAX NET                                          ~$48,000
```

**For 900–1,100 hours of your life.** That is **$44–53 per hour of total time**,
with capital at risk and no benefits.

**Downside** (45 days, one $6,000 failure): **$8,000–15,000, or a loss.**
**Most likely outcome: $20,000–$60,000 pre-tax for 800–1,200 hours.**

## 4 · 🔴 The actual disqualifier

**This consumes the founder, not the capital.**

Six hours a day in a cab, hearing protection on, 30–60 minutes from your desk.
**You cannot take a supplier call, quote a materials job, or visit a customer.**
A distribution business in year one is *entirely* relationship-building and
sourcing.

> **These two businesses compete for the same scarce resource — your attention
> during business hours — and they compete head-on.**

At $20K–$50K net for ~1,000 hours, that's **roughly $30 per founder-hour.** If an
hour spent on Supply is worth more than $30 — and in year one it almost certainly
is — **this is value-destroying even when it's profitable.**

## 5 · The market is saturating, with evidence

- A 15-year operator documents rates falling from **$350+/hr to $200–$225/hr** in
  a comparable market, saturation now *"as thick as the regular lawn care
  market."*
- **A Louisville-based lead-gen site already ranks for "Munfordville forestry
  mulching"** with a templated page for every town in Kentucky. Organic search in
  your own county is already being harvested by an outside operator with a
  marketing budget. *(Its published rates — skid steer mulcher **$2,500/day +
  $500 transport** — are still the real market signal: that's what your
  price-shopping customer sees.)*
- Southeast rates run **10–20% below** national. **Kentucky is not a premium
  market.**
- Barrier to entry is a machine and a trailer, and every trade publication for
  five years has said this is a good business.

**Quote by the day or the hour until you have 100+ hours of your own production
data.** A skid-steer-class machine in Kentucky hardwood does **0.5–1.5
acres/day** — per-acre quoting is where new operators lose their shirts.

## 6 · Kentucky specifics nobody warns you about

**⚠️ Class A CDL.** A properly rated rig — a ~10,250 lb CTL plus a 2,790 lb head
on a trailer that can legally carry it — puts you over **26,001 lbs GCWR with a
trailer over 10,000 lbs GVWR.** That is a **Class A CDL** plus a DOT medical
card. A **USDOT number is required at 10,001 lbs** and must be displayed; **KIT**
almost certainly applies. The workaround people use — lighter trailer, or plating
the truck under 26,000 — produces an *overloaded* trailer, which voids insurance
in a wreck and is exactly what a roadside inspector on I-65 is looking for.

**⚠️ Kentucky Master Logger.** KRS 149.330–149.355 requires a master logger **on
site and in charge at all times** on every commercial logging operation. **Whether
pure mulching with nothing sold counts is UNVERIFIED** — but the moment you sell
one sawlog off a clearing job you are almost certainly inside it. **The course is
3 days and $85. Cheapest risk elimination in this document.**

**⚠️ Karst.** Hart County sits in the **Mammoth Cave karst region**, and the
Hidden River Cave groundwater subbasin is already documented as severely impacted.
**A hydraulic hose failure or fuel spill into a sinkhole is direct injection into
a drinking-water aquifer** — plus collapse risk under a 10,000 lb machine. Carry
spill kit and absorbents.

**KYR10 stormwater** triggers at **one acre** of disturbance (or part of a larger
common plan). Permit reissued **3 January 2025**; NOI filed **7 days before**
construction; **fee is $0**; SWPPP with **inspections every 7 days**, or 14 days
plus within 24 hours of a 0.5" storm. **Civil penalties reach $25,000 per day.**
Get it in your contract: *who holds the KYR10.*

**Burning** is illegal within 150 feet of woodland **between 6 a.m. and 6 p.m.**
during **Feb 15 – Apr 30** and **Oct 1 – Dec 15** — nearly 4.5 months of
daytime-closed. *(Which is a strong commercial argument for mulching over
burn-and-haul.)*

**Power lines: 10 feet minimum approach.** Work inside that is line-clearance
tree trimming requiring qualified personnel. **You are not qualified.** A
mulching head on a raised loader arm reaches higher than people think.

**Kentucky 811** — two working days, and it applies on private property when
using mechanised equipment. The statutory fine is trivial ($250 first offence).
**The exposure is a cut fibre trunk at six figures, a struck gas line, and a
carrier that litigates coverage because you skipped a required locate.** 811 does
**not** locate private lines — get the customer to sign a private-facilities
disclosure.

## 7 · The site-work adjacency — I had this wrong

I previously wrote that land clearing is *"the front end of site development."*
**That is technically true and economically misleading.**

Site development on a small commercial pad is asphalt, concrete, storm and
sanitary, water and electric service, curbs, sidewalks, lighting and landscaping.
**Clearing is plausibly $3,000–$15,000 of a $229,000–$784,000 line** — and often
near zero, because these sites are frequently already-cleared outparcels.

> **"Land clearing is the front end of site development" is true the way "the
> driveway is the front end of a house."**

And the economics of moving up invert what you'd expect:

| | Clearing operator | Excavation / site sub |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $80K–$220K | $1M–$12M |
| **Net margin** | high but small | **5.5–7.5%** at $1M–5M |
| Startup to enter | owns machine | **$125,000–$750,000** |
| Bonding capacity | **effectively zero** | 3–5 yr of clean financials |

**Site work is lower-margin, higher-revenue, higher-risk.** The gate isn't
equipment, it's **GPS grade control ($25K–60K — you cannot hit commercial pad
tolerance by eye), working capital at net-45 to net-90 with retainage, and
bonding you won't have for years.**

> ### The uncomfortable observation
> **The distribution company is a better on-ramp to site work than the mulcher
> is.** Selling aggregate, pipe, geotextile or erosion control puts you in the
> room with every excavation contractor within sixty miles, teaches you who's
> good and who's slow-pay, and builds the exact relationships that later feed
> subcontract work — **with zero equipment capital.**
>
> **Clearing gives you a machine and a suntan. Distribution gives you the
> network.**

## 8 · The smallest sensible version

If the pull is real — and there is a real business here for *someone* — do it in
this order and **stop at any step that fails.**

**Step 0 · this week · free.** Model number, auxiliary GPM, factory high flow
yes/no. Written dealer quote for a retrofit.

**Step 1 · this week · free.** **Call five mulching contractors within 45 miles
as a customer.** Get day rate and lead time. **Under one week means saturated —
walk away. Four weeks or more means there's room.** This is the single biggest
gap in the research and only you can close it.

**Step 2 · 30–90 days · ~$5,000 at risk.** LLC, Hart County occupational licence,
GL + inland marine, one-page site, Google Business Profile. **Then go sell five
jobs at real prices — before owning a head.** If you can't sell five, you won't
sell fifty, and you learned it for $5,000 instead of $60,000.

**Step 3 · fulfil by RENTING.** Head rental runs **$450–$900/day**; a full
high-flow carrier + head package **$900–$1,550/day**. **Bill $2,500–$3,000/day
and keep $1,000–$1,600 of contribution with zero capital, zero payment in a dead
month, zero teeth cost, and zero exposure to a $12,000 hydraulic motor failure.**
Your margin is half of ownership. **You are buying optionality, and in year one
of a two-business household optionality is worth more than margin.**

**Step 4 · only after two full seasons hold.** Buy the head — and **hire the
operator rather than being him.** The moment it supports an operator who isn't
you, it stops competing for your attention and becomes the cash-generating asset
you actually wanted.

**Step 5 · parallel, free, high-leverage.** Get on the Hart County NRCS field
office and Division of Forestry service forester's radar. **NRCS EQIP pays
$9.23–$69.69/acre for brush management** — that does not pay *you*, it makes a
farmer who wouldn't spend $4,000 willing to spend it. **It is a sales tool, and
the relationship costs nothing.** Take the $85 Master Logger course.

> **🔴 What not to do:** buy a $41,000 head and a $25,000 retrofit in month one on
> the strength of a national daily average published by a marketing agency — then
> discover in February, mid-dead-season and mid-rain, that you owe $10,200 a year
> on it and your supplier relationships went cold while you were in the cab.
