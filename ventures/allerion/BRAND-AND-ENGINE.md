## 1 · Positioning

**The paragraph:**

> Allerion Technologies is the platform company behind an independent
> construction-supply house — the storefront, the compliance database that ties
> every SKU to the OSHA paragraph that requires it, the MCP agent interface at
> `mcp.allerion.io`, and Job Site Earth, the live jobsite layer — built for and
> proven on its own distributor first. Fastenal built FASTVend and its Digital
> Footprint in-house and will never sell it to you; Allerion is that stack in a
> form an independent can rent. The customer is the independent distributor
> Ferguson itself describes: the **10,000+ small-to-medium ($10–300M revenue)
> independent companies** serving a $340B North American construction market
> (Ferguson FY2025 IR materials, FY ended July 31, 2025 — via
> `DISTRIBUTION-MARKET.md` §1). Those firms are being bought at ~1.0x sales
> precisely because they have good customers and bad systems; Allerion exists so
> an independent can get the systems without selling the company.

**Why "proven on its own distributor first" is the whole strategy.** SiteOne —
the closest structural analogue to a Misty Valley-sized customer, at $4.7B —
reached only low-single-digit digital share and ~10,000 regular digital users
after years of investment (SiteOne FY2025 disclosures, via
`ECOMMERCE-LEADERS.md`). Fastenal's famous 61.4% "Digital Footprint" is mostly
vending machines and bins, not a web store (Fastenal FY2025 earnings release,
19 Jan 2026, via `ECOMMERCE-LEADERS.md`) — it is capital and floor presence a
small independent cannot buy. The honest pitch window for Allerion is the gap
between those two facts: the majors' digital advantage is real, in-house, and
not for sale; the independents' alternative today is a generic Shopify theme
with no compliance data, no branch model, no contract pricing, and no agent
interface. Allerion's proof is not a claim — it is a store you can use, a
server you can call, and one named, real customer whose numbers we can show
with permission.

**What Allerion is not** (say it on the site): not a marketplace, not a
payments platform (see the staged posture in doc 12 — Misty Valley's own Stripe
account is the merchant today; Allerion carries no platform payments exposure),
not an "AI company." It is distribution software with a compliance spine.

## 2 · Name architecture

Two companies, two sites, one footer line. The rules below exist because SBA
affiliation turns on control, not on branding — but sloppy branding is how you
end up *arguing* about control (doc 12, "The affiliation problem": 13 CFR
121.103; entities are affiliates when one controls or has the power to control
the other, and SBA counts receipts of the concern **plus all affiliates**).

### 2.1 The public relationship

- **`mistyvalleysupply.com`** is the store. Products, prices, branch, the
  citation search, the About page. Allerion appears exactly once per page:
  the shipped footer line, **"powered by Allerion"**, linking to allerion.io.
  Per `BRAND-PLATFORM.md` §6: Allerion is named in software, licensing and
  government contexts — "never as a co-brand on a hard hat."
- **`allerion.io`** is the software company, and per doc 12 its contents are
  already defined: **docs, the MCP endpoint at `mcp.allerion.io`, a status
  page, and the pitch to the second customer.** Add to that: the compliance
  reference library (§4a below) and a changelog. That is the whole site. No
  store, no cart, no product prices for gear.
- Correct sentence forms: *"Misty Valley Supply runs on the Allerion
  platform."* / *"Allerion Technologies licenses its platform to Misty Valley
  Supply, its first customer."* Both name a **licensing** relationship —
  vendor and customer, invoice between them.

### 2.2 Rules that keep the brands from blurring

1. **Separate voices.** Misty Valley talks like the guy who unloaded the truck
   (`BRAND-PLATFORM.md` §5). Allerion writes like good engineering
   documentation: declarative, versioned, present tense, no war stories. If a
   sentence would sound right read aloud at the counter in Bonnieville, it
   belongs to Misty Valley, not Allerion.
2. **No shared visual system.** No safety orange anywhere on allerion.io (§3).
   No Allerion mark on Misty Valley trucks, invoices, PPE, or packaging. The
   only crossover artifact is the footer line, set in Misty Valley's own
   typography, not Allerion's.
3. **Case-study discipline.** Misty Valley is Allerion's reference customer
   and may be named as such — with real numbers only, with written permission,
   and always as a *customer*. Phrases that are banned on allerion.io because
   they imply one firm: "our store," "our warehouse," "our trucks," "our
   distributor," "sister company," "our retail arm," "the Allerion family of
   companies." Correct: "our first customer, Misty Valley Supply."
4. **Product ownership stays straight.** The Yard and Job Site Earth are
   *products of Misty Valley Supply, built by Allerion* on the storefront side
   (`BRAND-PLATFORM.md` §6) — but the software IP is Allerion's (doc 12). On
   allerion.io they are listed as platform capabilities ("marketplace module,"
   "jobsite layer"), not as Allerion consumer brands. One product, two correct
   descriptions, zero shared marketing.
5. **The govcon caution, in writing.** Allerion is registered on SAM.gov.
   Nothing on allerion.io may state or imply that Misty Valley and Allerion
   are one firm, share employees, share facilities, or share revenue beyond
   the license. The license is priced at arm's length and the pricing
   rationale is documented (doc 12's two directives: the affiliation question
   goes to a government-contracts attorney with real ownership on the table;
   the 70%-of-receipts presumption is a bright line to stay away from
   deliberately — which is itself a *business* reason Allerion needs a second
   customer, not just a marketing reason).
6. **Domains and email.** Joey's Allerion correspondence comes from
   @allerion.io; Misty Valley correspondence from the store's domain. No
   shared signature blocks. Small, boring, and exactly the kind of hygiene an
   auditor looks at first.

## 3 · Visual identity direction

**The premise:** a technology company that lives next door to a supply house.
Misty Valley owns safety orange (`--safety: 21 94% 46%` / #e45407, warm paper
grounds, big coarse retail type — see `brand/ART-DIRECTION.md`). Allerion must
read as its complement, not its sibling: cold where Misty Valley is warm, quiet
where the store is loud, precise where the store is friendly. The reference
image is not a SaaS landing page; it is an instrument panel and a title block
on a drawing set.

### 3.1 Palette

Cold, ink-forward, one working accent. All values HSL (hex for reference):

| Token | HSL | Hex (approx) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--ground` | `220 20% 98%` | #f7f8fa | page ground (cold white — vs. Misty Valley's warm #f8f8f6) |
| `--panel` | `0 0% 100%` | #ffffff | cards, code blocks on light |
| `--ink` | `222 30% 11%` | #141924 | headings, body |
| `--ink-2` | `222 12% 38%` | #565d6d | secondary text |
| `--rule` | `220 14% 88%` | #dde0e6 | hairlines |
| **`--allerion`** | `214 55% 22%` | #193a57 | brand primary — a Prussian/ink blue, drawn from drafting ink, not from SaaS blue |
| **`--signal`** | `199 90% 42%` | #0b98cc | the one accent — links, live data, interactive; a drafting cyan |
| `--operational` | `152 65% 38%` | #22a06b | status page "operational" only — never decorative |
| `--fault` | `4 72% 46%` | #ca2318 | incidents/errors only |
| Dark ground | `222 28% 8%` | #0f131c | docs/status dark mode; dark-capable from day one |

Rules: the accent appears on well under 10% of any page (the QXO lesson from
`ART-DIRECTION.md` §2: pick one accent, spend 95% of the page on neutrals).
Green and red are *semantic*, reserved for the status page and error states.
**Orange of any temperature is banned** — it is Misty Valley's.

### 3.2 Type (Google Fonts)

- **Instrument Sans** — display, UI, and body. A grotesque with just enough
  drawn character to not be Inter; the name is a free gift to the brand story.
  Weights 400/500/700. Tight, small scale: this is a documentation site, not a
  retail page, so 15–16px body, 24/32/44 display steps, letter-spacing 0.
- **JetBrains Mono** — code, endpoints, version numbers, citations, tables of
  terms. Anywhere the machine-readable thing is the content. 13–14px, never
  all-caps-tracked-out (the `ART-DIRECTION.md` §1.1 failure is not repeated
  here).
- Fallback stacks required (`system-ui` / `ui-monospace`). No serif face; no
  condensed display face; nothing "industrial."

### 3.3 Logo direction (described, not drawn)

An **alerion** is a real heraldic charge: an eagle displayed, stylized without
beak or claws. The name is sitting on its own mark. Direction: a small
geometric alerion — the bird reduced to 3–5 precise strokes, constructed on a
visible grid, monoweight, drawn the way a survey symbol or an aeronautical
chart glyph is drawn. Rendered in `--allerion` ink or reversed white; never
gradient-filled, never contained in a rounded app-icon squircle. Wordmark
"Allerion" in Instrument Sans Medium, sentence case, set beside (not under)
the mark. The lockup's descriptor line, in JetBrains Mono, is the positioning
in five words: `distribution software · compliance spine`.

### 3.4 The one motif

**Registration marks.** The small crosshair/tick marks used to align a drawing
set or calibrate a plot — used sparingly as section anchors, on the corners of
diagrams, and as the status page's pulse glyph. It encodes the whole company:
alignment between the physical jobsite and the data layer (Job Site Earth), and
between a product and its rule (the citation database). One motif, used small.
It is not decoration on every surface; it appears where a figure or a live
number appears.

### 3.5 What it must NOT look like

- **No generic SaaS kit:** no purple-to-blue gradient heroes, no glassmorphic
  cards, no floating 3D blobs, no fake dashboard screenshots at an angle.
- **No AI-slop tropes:** no sparkle glyphs (✨), no "magic" language, no robot
  imagery, no neural-net node-and-edge wallpaper, no chat-bubble mascots.
- **No borrowed trade dress:** no safety orange, no hi-vis yellow, no hard-hat
  iconography, no distressed slab caps, no flag — that whole register belongs
  to Misty Valley's category and is banned there too (`BRAND-PLATFORM.md` §9,
  cliché #5). Allerion selling "toughness" would be a company that writes
  software cosplaying a company that carries bundles.
- **No stock photography at all.** Screens are shown as real screenshots at
  100% scale; the jobsite is shown only via Job Site Earth's own map renders.
  If we don't have the image, we don't fake the image.

## 4 · The revenue engine (with Joey minimally involved)

### 4a · Traffic before affiliates — the order of operations, corrected

**The correction:** affiliate revenue is not earned by *becoming an affiliate*
— sign-ups are free and instant, and doc 14 already proved the point: 15% of a
$59.99 course is $9. Affiliate revenue is earned by **owning traffic that is
about to buy something**. Links without an audience pay nothing; an audience
makes any link pay. So the engine is built in this order: reference content →
indexed pages → qualified traffic → *then* monetization layers (affiliate,
seats, store, and ultimately Allerion demos).

**The asset already exists.** The store carries ~24 SKUs each with a written
"what the rule requires" paragraph (`/home/user/mvs-store/src/data.ts` — the
`why:` field ties, e.g., a guardrail section to 1926.501(b)(10) and the
200 lb/42 in numbers of 1926.502(b)) plus citation-first search ("search
1926.501," `BRAND-PLATFORM.md` §1). Today those paragraphs live inside a React
PWA where crawlers barely see them. The move: publish them as **static,
indexable reference pages** — one page per OSHA paragraph and consensus
standard (`/rules/1926.501`, `/rules/ansi-z359.11`), each carrying the plain-
language explanation, the numbers, the honest "what it does not cover"
sentence, and the products that satisfy it. The store's compliance library
becomes the corridor's reference shelf. (Where these pages live —
mistyvalleysupply.com for commercial pages, with the compliance *dataset*
itself an Allerion product — follows the §2 ownership rules: the store
publishes the pages; Allerion owns and licenses the data and the machinery
that maintains it.)

**The demand evidence — what can and cannot be verified:**

- Exact keyword search volumes are **not publicly verifiable without paid
  tools** (Ahrefs/Semrush volumes are estimates behind paywalls; I will not
  quote a number I cannot source). The free verification step is Google
  Keyword Planner under the store's Google Ads account — a [automated]-tagged
  task in §6, one hour.
- The verifiable proxy is enforcement and mandate data, which is strong:
  **1926.501 is the most-cited OSHA standard in America** — 7,271 citations in
  FY2023, and OSHA issued 5,914 fall-protection citations in FY2025 with a
  maximum willful penalty now $165,514 (reported via nes-ehs.com and
  workplacepub.com summaries of OSHA data, checked 5 Sep 2026 — secondary
  sources; the underlying Top-10 list is published annually by OSHA/NSC).
  Every one of those citations is a company that searched, or should have
  searched, exactly the query our pages answer.
- Statutory forced demand for training queries: NYC Local Law 196 (SST cards),
  Nevada NRS 618.983 (OSHA 10 within 15 days of hire), Missouri RSMo 292.675
  (public works) — all verified in doc 14 §4, checked 5 Sep 2026.

**The multiplier: structured data.** `AGENTIC-COMMERCE.md` §7 already ordered
this for agent-readability, and it is the same work SEO needs: schema.org
`Product`/`Offer` JSON-LD on every product page (real `mpn`/`sku`, no
fabricated GTINs), `Organization` + `LocalBusiness` with the Bonnieville
service area, and — for the rule pages — `FAQPage`/`TechArticle` markup so the
citation answers are eligible for rich results and answer-engine extraction
(Perplexity/Gemini read the schema substrate; doc 16 §7). One implementation
serves three consumers: Google, shopping surfaces, and agents. This is the
highest-leverage automated task in the whole plan.

**Honest constraint:** a new domain ranking for competitive commercial terms
("OSHA 30 online") against 360training, ClickSafety and OSHA.gov is a
multi-year fight we will mostly lose. The winnable queries are the long tail
the big providers do not write for: specific citation paragraphs, specific
gear-to-rule questions ("does a warning line need flags," "skylight cover
requirements"), Kentucky/Tennessee state-plan specifics. Low volume each,
high intent, near-zero competition, and they compound.

### 4d · Realistic numbers — and what "millions from affiliate links" actually requires

**Traffic ramp (planning estimates, not promises — no analytics history exists
yet):** a new reference library shipping 2–4 pages/week with correct schema
typically sees near-zero months 1–3 (index lag), low hundreds of monthly
organic sessions by month 6, and — if the long-tail thesis holds — 1,000–5,000
monthly sessions by months 12–18. These are order-of-magnitude planning
figures; the first real number replaces them.

**Monetization density:** display networks run **$10–30 RPM** (revenue per
1,000 sessions; Mediavine/AdSense ranges — adstimate.com and kalkulero.com
benchmark pages, checked 5 Sep 2026, secondary sources). Affiliate EPC
benchmarks run **$0.45–$1.50 blended**, higher in insurance/SaaS (Partnero /
affiliate-benchmark roundups, checked 5 Sep 2026 — low-authority sources;
treat as directional). A high-intent niche site doing everything right might
blend **$20–50 RPM** across display + affiliate. Applied honestly:

- **5,000 sessions/month × $30 RPM ≈ $150/month.** That is year-one reality:
  beer money that also happens to be a growing organic sales channel for
  seats, gear, and — the real prize — inbound Allerion interest.
- **"Millions" (say $1M/year) at even a generous $30 blended RPM requires
  ~33 million sessions/year — roughly 2.8–4 million visits per month,
  sustained.** That is top-tier trade-publisher scale (think of it as
  building a construction-media company with an editorial staff), reached by
  a handful of sites per niche after years and a seven-figure content
  investment. It is not a side effect of adding links to a store.
- The corrected ambition: the content engine's job is **not** to make
  millions in commissions. Its job is (1) low-hundreds/month of automated
  revenue that scales with zero marginal Joey-hours, (2) qualified store and
  training traffic (a $2,700-gross OSHA 10 class booked from a rule page
  beats a thousand $9 commissions), and (3) the public proof that the
  Allerion platform generates demand — which is worth more in the 2027 pitch
  than the commissions themselves. The millions, if they come, come from
  platform licenses.

## 5 · The minimally-involved operating model

What runs without Joey, what needs him, and the boundary between them. The
design principle is inherited from Misty Valley's value #5 ("Nothing lives in
one man's head") applied to a software company: every recurring task is either
automated or documented well enough that it could be handed off.

**Runs without Joey:**

1. **Content pipeline — agent-drafted, human-approved.** An agent drafts each
   rule page from the CFR text and the existing `why:` paragraphs; checks
   citations against the live eCFR; queues the draft. A human (Joey or,
   eventually, a paid reviewer) approves in a 30-minute weekly batch — the
   approval is the only human step, and it is non-negotiable: a wrong
   compliance claim is worse than no page (`BRAND-PLATFORM.md` value #1: a
   stale citation is worse than none; the annual citation-maintenance duty is
   the same pipeline pointed at old pages).
2. **The store's automation** — orders, quotes (the `quote_roofscreen` engine),
   RFQ intake (`create_quote_request`, doc 16 §8), roster/seat tracking, all
   already specified; the affiliate links and schema ride on pages that
   maintain themselves.
3. **The listings as inbound demos.** Claude Connectors Directory and ChatGPT
   App Directory (doc 16 §§6–7): the server already speaks MCP; submission is
   review-cycle work, then the listings sit there as permanent, zero-marginal-
   cost proof. Every directory visitor who tries the quoting tool has watched
   an Allerion demo with nobody presenting it. Expectations per doc 16:
   discovery is weak and name-weighted; these are demo assets and RFQ intake,
   not a sales channel.
4. **The status page and uptime record** — an automated marketing asset:
   twelve unattended months of green is a slide in the 2027 deck.
5. **Monitoring** — search console, affiliate dashboards, intake-queue alerts,
   all summarized into one weekly automated digest.

**Always needs Joey (the honest list):**

1. **Trainer classes, once authorized.** The OSHA Outreach authorization is
   personal and non-delegable — Joey the person, not any company, owns the
   records, OTPRs and card duties (OTP Requirements 2024 §V.P, via doc 14).
   Two days per class, and per doc 14 it is the best-paid marketing on the
   calendar: ~$2,200–2,400 net per class *and* twenty buyers in a room.
2. **Relationship sales.** The 2027 second-distributor deal will be closed by
   a founder talking to an owner — probably an aging independent with good
   customers and bad systems (`DISTRIBUTION-MARKET.md` §4.6). No pipeline
   automation replaces that call, and pretending otherwise is how the one
   deal that matters gets dropped.

**The human queue that must never silently rot:** the RFQ/intake inbox. Doc 16
said it plainly — an unanswered "design it with us" request is worse than no
app. It needs a named owner and an SLA (one business day), and that owner can
be Ben's side of the house since the requests are Misty Valley trade.
