## ⚠️ Before anything else — settle the legal names

The documents in this folder say **Misty Valley Contracting.** You have been
saying **Misty Valley Construction.** Those are different companies as far as
Kentucky, a bank, a bond agent and a court are concerned.

**Pull the exact registered names from the Kentucky Secretary of State
(sos.ky.gov, Business Search — free, two minutes) and use them verbatim
everywhere from here forward.** A holdco structure built on the wrong name is a
holdco structure that does not hold anything. `01-entity-structure.md` has the
rest of the formation work.

## 1 · The rule that governs everything below

> **EOS runs at the operating company. Not at the holding company.**

This is the mistake almost everyone makes with a holdco. They build a third
layer of meetings, rocks and scorecards over two businesses that each already
need their own — and the group spends its Mondays reporting upward instead of
selling and shipping.

**Each operating company gets its own complete EOS.** Its own V/TO,
accountability chart, scorecard, rocks and weekly Level 10.

**The holding company gets three things and nothing else:**

1. **One set of core values, shared by both companies.**
2. **Capital allocation** — where the money goes.
3. **The firewall** between the two companies.

That is the entire job. **A holdco that starts setting rocks for its operating
companies has stopped being a holdco and started being middle management.**

framing company and the supply company have different definitions of "everybody
gets paid" is a group that will eventually tear along that seam. The six values
in `CONSTITUTION.md` are the group's values.

## 4 · Capital allocation — the holdco's actual job

This is where a holding company earns its existence. Four decisions, made
quarterly, by Christopher with two-of-three agreement:

1. **How much cash does Construction need to run, and how much can it release?**
2. **How much does Supply need to hold its inventory and receivables?**
   *(Peak cash need modelled at **$499K–$708K** depending on structure. Construction
   cannot fund that. Say so out loud.)*
3. **Whose balance sheet does the debt sit on, and whose guarantee backs it?**
4. **What does not get funded this quarter.** ← the one people skip

> **The rule: Construction's cash may fund Supply's growth, but Construction's
> ability to make payroll is never the variable.** A framing company that misses
> payroll to float a supply company's container has destroyed a ten-year business
> to protect a one-year-old one.

## 5 · The Enterprises scorecard — five numbers, monthly

Not weekly. The holdco does not need a weekly pulse.

| # | Measurable | Owner | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Consolidated cash on hand** | Christopher | ≥ 8 weeks of combined payroll |
| 2 | Construction net cash generated | Christopher | positive, every month |
| 3 | Supply net cash consumed | Christopher | ≤ the quarterly allocation |
| 4 | **Supply revenue from customers other than Construction** | Joey | ≥ 50% by end of 2027 |
| 5 | **Related-party transactions documented before the sale** | Joey | **100%. No exceptions.** |

**Number 4 is the one that decides whether you own two companies or one company
with a department.** Number 5 is the one that keeps you out of court.

## 6 · The firewall — Joey's seat

Two companies under one owner, one selling to the other, with a partner employed
by a general contractor that hires the first one. **Every one of those is legal.
All three undocumented at once is how good families end up in depositions.**

**The three rules, and they are absolute:**

1. **Every sale from Supply to Construction is priced at arm's length and
   documented in writing before it happens.** Not reconstructed at year end.
   `01-entity-structure.md` has the protocol; a Kentucky construction attorney
   signs off on it before the first invoice.
2. **Kelley Construction's information never crosses into either company.** Not
   bid numbers, not the pipeline, not sub pricing. Joey's *skill* is his;
   **his employer's information never is.** This is Article IX and it is
   unamendable.
3. **The two companies keep separate books, separate bank accounts, separate
   invoices, and separate signatures.** *Inter-Tel v. Linn Station Properties*
   (Ky. 2012) is the case that decides whether a Kentucky court respects the
   separation. It looks at exactly these facts.

**Joey owns this seat because he is the one with the conflict.** The person with
the exposure runs the control. That is not a punishment, it is how controls work.

## 7 · The meeting pulse for the group

| Meeting | Who | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Supply L10** | Ben · Christopher · Joey | Weekly, 90 min |
| **Construction L10** | Ben · Christopher | Weekly, 90 min |
| **Enterprises Quarterly** | all three | Half a day, every 90 days |
| **Enterprises Annual** | all three | One day |

### 🔴 But not yet. Start with ONE meeting.

Three people, two companies, no employees, and Supply has not placed a purchase
order. **Two weekly Level 10s for three people is theatre.**

**Run one L10 for the group, Monday mornings, starting 6 October 2026.** Split
into two the day either of these is true:

- **Supply has three or more employees who are not partners**, or
- **The single agenda regularly runs past 90 minutes.**

**Write that trigger down now**, so that splitting is a decision you already made
rather than an argument you have later.
