# The Misty Valley Operating System

**EOS applied to Misty Valley Supply.** Bonnieville, Kentucky.
Drafted 5 September 2026 · **not yet adopted**

*EOS — the Entrepreneurial Operating System, from Gino Wickman's* Traction —
*organises a company around six components: Vision, People, Data, Issues,
Process and Traction. What follows is that framework applied to this business
with real numbers, in our own words. Where the framework does not fit yet, §9
says so plainly rather than pretending.*

# PART ONE — THE VISION/TRACTION ORGANIZER

## 1 · Core Values

Straight from the constitution, cut to the handles you can say in a hiring
interview. **These are the hiring, firing and review instrument** — every person
gets rated + / +/− / − on each, and three-strikes applies to the values, not to
performance.

| | Value | The behavioral test |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Sell the labor, not the steel** | Did he try to win it on service, or on price? |
| **2** | **What we said is in the bundle** | Did he measure it, or take the cert on faith? |
| **3** | **The date is the product** | Did he give the bad number early or the good number late? |
| **4** | **Everybody gets paid** | When cash was tight, who did he pay first? |
| **5** | **Go and look** | When did he last stand in the plant or on the deck? |
| **6** | **Nobody's name is bigger than the company** | Who else can now do the thing he does? |

## 2 · Core Focus

**Purpose:** *Make the crew's day easier than it was yesterday.*

**Niche:** *Cut, labeled, sequenced cold-formed steel framing packages,
delivered by floor and by phase.*

Everything gets tested against these two lines. **If a proposed piece of business
does not serve the purpose or sit in the niche, the answer is no — even when it
is profitable.** That is the entire discipline; it is also the hardest one.

## 3 · Ten-Year Target — 31 December 2036

> **No commercial steel framing package between Louisville and Nashville gets
> bought without somebody asking us to price it first.**
>
> **$50M in revenue.**

The number is a **target, not a forecast.** It is reachable from the modelled
direct-ship structure — which reaches $16.7M in year three at ~35-day cash
conversion — but only if the distributor appointment in `10-the-channel-problem.md`
comes through. **Without that appointment, this number is fiction.** State it
that way to a lender.

## 4 · Marketing Strategy

**The List — who we actually sell to:**

- **Geographic:** the I-65 corridor. Louisville · Elizabethtown · Bardstown ·
  Bowling Green · Nashville. Ninety minutes from Bonnieville in either direction.
- **Demographic:** CFS framing subcontractors, and GCs self-performing metal
  stud. Interiors, light commercial, QSR, retail, medical office, mid-rise.
- **Psychographic:** the ones who have been burned by a date. **A buyer who only
  wants tonnage is not on this list, and chasing him teaches us the wrong
  business.**

**The Three Uniques:**

1. **We take the job off before the buyout.**
2. **It arrives cut, labeled and sequenced by floor and by phase.**
3. **We measure the steel — and can prove it ten years later.**

Uniques must be true, provable, and hard to copy. All three are. Number three is
the one nobody else in the corridor will match, because it costs money and
creates liability for anyone who claims it falsely.

**The Proven Process — "The Deck Process," seven steps:**

```
  1  READ AND PRICE THE SET      takeoff from the wall schedule, at bid stage
  2  CONFIRM THE ASSEMBLY        UL design, gauge, listing — before we quote
  3  BUY THE LOAD                sourcing is a decision, not an identity
  4  VERIFY AT RECEIPT           micrometer, coating stripped, readings logged
  5  CUT · LABEL · BUNDLE        by floor, by phase, by the crew's sequence
  6  DELIVER TO THE DECK         on the day we said
  7  CLOSE OUT                   documentation package, traceable to the lot
```

Draw it on one page. Show it to every customer. **It is a sales tool before it is
an operations tool** — a buyer who sees step 4 understands instantly why we are
not the cheapest.

**The Guarantee — two candidates:**

| | The guarantee | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | *On the deck on the day we said, or you don't pay the freight.* | Strong, but **model the exposure before publishing it.** One bad quarter of carrier failure eats the margin on a lot of loads. |
| B | **Every bundle ships with its thickness readings. If any piece measures below 95% of design thickness, we replace the load and eat the freight.** | ✅ **Lead with this one.** It is on our strongest ground, no competitor will copy it, and if the process in step 4 works it costs approximately nothing. |

### GWC — and the finding that matters

EOS asks three questions of every person in every seat. **All three must be yes.
Two out of three is a no.**

| | **G**ets it | **W**ants it | **C**apacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ben** — Visionary | ✅ | ✅ | ⚠️ also running Contracting |
| **Christopher** — Integrator | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| **Joey** — Sales & Marketing | ✅ | ✅ | 🔴 **No** |
| Operations | — | — | 🔴 **vacant** |

> ### 🔴 Joey does not have Capacity, and the system will not work until that is settled.
>
> He is **chief estimator at a general contractor.** In the week this company was
> founded he worked a full field week on two jobs and a second shift to midnight
> — for his employer. That is not a scheduling problem, it is the C in GWC, and
> EOS is blunt about it: **a seat held by someone without capacity is a vacant
> seat with a name on it.**
>
> It is compounded by Article IX of the constitution, which correctly forbids him
> from bringing his employer's information through the door. **His capacity is
> not just limited — parts of it are fenced.**
>
> **Three honest options. Pick one before the next quarter, not after:**
>
> **A. Bound the seat.** Joey owns takeoff and quoting for a stated number of
> hours a week, on nights and weekends, on a defined customer list outside
> Kelley's market. Written down. Everyone agrees it is a part-time seat and the
> scorecard targets are sized to it.
>
> **B. Hire into it.** Joey stays as owner and advisor; someone else holds the
> Sales seat. Costs money the company does not have yet.
>
> **C. He goes full time.** The only version that unlocks the ten-year target —
> and it is not a Q4 2026 decision. It is the decision after the appointment
> lands and the book supports a salary.
>
> **Doing nothing is choosing A badly.**

## 11 · Process — the handful to document

EOS says a company runs on a small number of core processes, documented at a high
level and **Followed By All.** Six here. Twenty pages total, not two hundred.

1. **The Takeoff Process** — plans in, priced package out.
2. **The Purchasing Process** — including the AD/CVD and classification check.
3. **The Receiving Process** — ⭐ *the micrometer protocol. Write this one first.
   It is the only process that can end the company if it is skipped.*
4. **The Cut & Bundle Process** — labeling and sequencing convention.
5. **The Delivery Process** — scheduling, the deck, proof of delivery.
6. **The Collections Process** — credit application, PG, terms, escalation.

## 12 · Traction — the meeting pulse

| Meeting | When | Format |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 10** | **Same day, same time, every week. 90 minutes.** | Scorecard (5) · Rock review (5) · Customer and employee headlines (5) · To-dos (5) · **IDS (60)** · Conclude (5) |
| **Quarterly** | Full day, every 90 days | Review the quarter · set the next Rocks · clear the issues list |
| **Annual** | Two days, once a year | Rebuild the three-year picture and the one-year plan. **Read the constitution out loud.** |

The Level 10 is the whole system. **Sixty of its ninety minutes is solving
issues** — not reporting, not updating. If your meeting is mostly updates, you
are having a status call and calling it EOS.

**Start it Monday 6 October 2026, 6:30 a.m., before the field starts.** Three
people. Ninety minutes. Christopher runs it.

## 13 · Where EOS does not fit yet — be honest about it

EOS assumes an operating company with revenue, employees and a weekly rhythm.
**Misty Valley Supply has three partners, no employees, and has not placed its
first purchase order.** So:

| Component | Status |
|---|---|
| **Vision** | ✅ Install now. This document is most of it. |
| **Data** | ✅ Install now — Rock 6. Ten numbers work at any size. |
| **Issues** | ✅ Install now. The list already exists. |
| **Traction** | ✅ Install now. Three people can hold an L10. |
| **People** | ⚠️ Chart drawn; **one seat vacant and one has no capacity.** Real once there is a hire. |
| **Process** | ⚠️ Write #3 (Receiving) now. The rest are written by doing the work. |

**And one limit worth naming:** EOS is a management system, not a strategy. It
will make you superb at executing whatever you point it at. It has nothing to say
about whether the distributor appointment lands, whether the corridor recovers,
or whether importing is a good idea. **Those answers are in the research files,
not in this one.** Do not mistake a green scorecard for a good business.
