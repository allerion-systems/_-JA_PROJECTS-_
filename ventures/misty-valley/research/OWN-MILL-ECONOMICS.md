# Own-Mill Economics — Panel Line vs. Stud Line for Misty Valley Supply

**Research date: 5 September 2026.** Companion to `CHINA-STUD-IMPORT.md` (duty
stack and stud-certification findings, verified 2026-09-04/05 — carried over
here, not re-derived). Question: should Misty Valley (Bonnieville, KY) buy
roll-forming machinery to make its own metal roof/wall **panels** and/or
cold-formed steel **studs**, instead of buying from Superior Metals (fab) and
Dutchman Metal (Liberty, KY — Mennonite mill, same-day panels)?

**Marking convention:** **[V]** = verified against a live listing, published
document, or dated trade source (cited inline). **[E]** = estimate — quote-only
market, triangulated, or dated example. Machinery is a quote-driven market;
treat every [E] as "make the phone call."

**One-line answer up front:** a real ag-panel capability is cheaper than the
folklore says — **$48K–$105K [V]** buys a working 36" ag/PBR machine plus
uncoiler (new import-class or lightly used US), while a full MRS/Bradbury-class
in-plant line runs **$250K–$900K+ [E/V]** — and the panel spread (~$0.60/LF
gross, MRS's own published example [V-2018]) pays back the small machine at
**~100 squares/month** and the big line only at Dutchman-scale volume
(500–800+ sq/mo). The stud line fails on the same gate the China container
did: certification, not machinery. **Panel line: yes, later, small, probably
used. Stud line: no.**

## 2. Portable / entry machines + the trim shop every panel operation needs

- **New Tech Machinery SSQ II MultiPro** (standing seam): **$120K–$130K
  average, profile-dependent [V — Western States Metal Roofing comparison,
  2026]**. Note: **SSQ II is discontinued 30 Sep 2026**; successor **SSQ3**
  runs the same 16 profiles with 25-min QWIKSwap changeovers [V]. NTM portable
  range overall $60K–$150K+ [V — NTM's own 2026 cost article].
- **Can a portable do ag panel?** Not an NTM standing-seam machine — SSQ-class
  machines form standing-seam/board-batten/flush profiles, not 36"
  exposed-fastener ag/PBR. But portable-format **ag/tuff-rib machines do
  exist** (CSC 16-station $47,850 [V]; Zimmerman Z-panel portable $103K used
  listing [V]; various HC/Gentech imports). Practical answer: ag panel is an
  in-plant product; the machine can be trailer-mounted but nobody chases it
  around jobsites — 20+ ft panels want a run-out and a rack truck.
- **Trim equipment** (no panel business survives without trim): CNC folder —
  **Roper Whitney AutoBrake / AutoMax class $20K–$45K motorized, $60K–$120K+
  CNC [V — industry buyer's-guide ranges, 2026]**; **Cidan** power folders
  from **~$50K, to $150K+ [V — dealer/surplus listings]**; **Jorns**-class
  twin-bender long folders **$200K+ [V — buyer's-guide range]**. Plus a
  slitter for trim coil ($15K–$40K [E]) or buy pre-slit flat stock. Sheffield
  Metals' startup-cost article (Nov 2024) [V]: brake $10K–$75K+, shear
  $50K–$100K+, rollformer $80K–$150K+, forklift $40K–$75K+, racking $10K–$40K+,
  ≥$200K all-in before coil.

### Duty on imported roll-forming **machinery**, September 2026

**Classification:** roll-forming machines classify under **HTS 8462**
(machine tools for working metal by bending/folding/roll-forming) —
**8462.25.00** numerically controlled roll forming machines, 8462.29 non-NC
[V — Flexport/UNIS HTS data; CBP machine-tool ICP]. 8455 is *rolling mills*
(reducing thickness) — generally the wrong heading for a profile roll-former,
though sellers sometimes ship under 8455.22; classification is a broker/
binding-ruling question.

| Layer | Rate | Status |
|---|---|---|
| MFN, 8462.25.00 | **4.4% [V — Flexport HTS]** | ordinary duty |
| **Section 301 List 1** (9903.88.01) | **+25% [V that List 1 covers industrial machinery/machine tools at 25%; E that no exclusion applies to your exact 10-digit line — confirm at entry]** | List 1 ($34B, 818 lines incl. machine tools) in force since Jul 2018; not raised in the 4-year review |
| **Section 232** | **$0 on the machine itself as of 5 Sep 2026 — with a caveat [E/V]** | 232 covers steel *articles and derivatives*, not machine tools per se. The Aug 18 2025 derivative expansion (407 codes) reached into Ch. 84 but the published additions are things like 8419/8431 parts and **8455.30.00 (mill rolls — a steel part, not a machine)** [V — CSMS #65936570 / AWPA steelHTSlist 08-15-25]. The **June 8 2026 "Annex C" equipment tariff** (25%, tiered 10/15% for US-metal content / partner countries) covers *agricultural and industrial equipment* — metalworking machine tools are **not named** in any coverage summary reviewed [V — Dimerco, Western Overseas, Schayer advisories], but the inclusions process adds codes 3×/year. **Broker must screen 8462.25/29 against the current 232 derivative + Annex C lists at entry.** |
| IEEPA / §122 | $0 — struck down / expired (per `CHINA-STUD-IMPORT.md` [V]) | |
| MPF/HMF | ~0.47% | FY2027 fee change 1 Oct 2026 |

**Planning number: ~30% duty on a Chinese machine** (4.4 + 25 + fees) [E];
worst case ~50%+ if a future inclusions round captures 8462. On a $12K panel
line that's ~$3.6K — duty is *not* what kills the Chinese-machine option.

**Freight + rigging:** a panel line ships in one 40ft container —
**Shanghai→USEC $9,587 [V — Drewry WCI 3 Sep 2026, carried from
`CHINA-STUD-IMPORT.md`]**; dray to Bonnieville $2,000–2,400 [E]; broker/port
~$700 [E]; unload/rig/level/wire (crane or big forklift, 3-phase hookup)
**$5K–15K [E]**. Landed, a $12K Chinese panel line is **~$28K–35K [E]** — still
half the price of the cheapest US iron.

**The honest support/spares risk [E, but well-documented pattern]:** no US
technician network — commissioning is you plus a manual (or $3–5K/week plus
airfare for a factory tech, visa permitting); electrics often need panel
rework to meet a KY inspector/NEC (Delta 380V 50Hz motors, non-UL components);
roll tooling is soft on the cheapest machines and re-cutting rolls means
shipping to China or paying a US toolroom more than the machine cost; PLC
programs are undocumented. Budget the machine price *again* in first-year
fixes, and assume 2–6 weeks down waiting on any broken custom part. The HC
(Huachen) imports Red River sells split the difference: Chinese iron with a
Texas dealer behind it — that's the sane way to buy Chinese, and it's how
much of the low end of the US panel market actually runs.

## 4. Stud/track lines — dedicated CFS roll-formers

- **Chinese dedicated stud/track:** $6,000–9,900/set drywall-gauge (Zhongke,
  Wadjay — §3 [V]); automated 30 m/min class $45K+ [V range]. Structural-gauge
  (33–68 mil) C-shape lines with punching run $30K–80K [E].
- **North American custom builders** — **Bradbury** (Moundridge KS), **Samco**
  (Toronto), **Dahlstrom Roll Form** (Jamestown NY): all quote-only;
  purpose-built stud/track lines with punching, flying cutoff and quick-change
  land **$250K–$750K+ [E]** (trade-guide "industrial systems $150K–$500K+"
  band [V — MachineMatcher startup guide] plus punching/changeover adders).
- **Integrated light-gauge framing systems** (roll-former + CAD-driven punch/
  screw-pilot/labeling — makes framed *panels*, not commodity studs):
  **FRAMECAD** (F325iT — residential/light commercial, to 18 ga [V spec]),
  **Howick** (FRAMA 3200 — up to 2,952 ft/hr, 22–18 ga [V spec]),
  **Vertex/Scottsdale Construction Systems** (Scotpanel/Scottruss; 25% down
  starts manufacture; financing partners to $750K zero-down [V — SCS
  financing/lead-time article, Apr 2026]), **Valtek** (V120–V300, 22–11 ga, to
  40 m/min [V spec]). **None publish prices**; Valtek states outright "there
  is no list price" [V]. Reported/positioning range for an installed system:
  **mid-six figures — roughly $250K–$700K+ [E; "entry-level LGS machines
  start in the mid-six-figure range" per trade summary]**. Used comps exist
  (FRAMECAD 325iT 2021, TF550H 2019 on Machinio — POA [V listings]).
- **Quick-change / dual-profile:** web-width adjustment (one machine, 3-5/8"
  → 6") is the option that matters; Bradbury/Samco offer automated width
  change, Chinese machines mostly fixed-width per set — a second profile =
  a second $8K machine, which is genuinely how the cheap shops do it [E/V].
- **Slit coil requirement:** a stud line eats **slit** structural galvanized
  (A653 SS Gr 33/50, G60/G90) in exact widths (e.g. ~7.6" for a 3-5/8"
  drywall stud) — you either buy slit multiples from a service center
  (Kloeckner Cincinnati: prepaint+bare slitting to 72" Braner line, serves
  KY [V]; Steel Warehouse, South Bend IN; Majestic Steel) or add a $100K+
  slitting line. Small shops buy slit. Slitting adds **$30–80/ton [V —
  general-steels 2026 guide]**.

## 5. Coil supply for a small mill — where the Amish advantage actually lives

**How it works [V process / E numbers]:** painted Galvalume/galvanized
"roofing coil" is bought either (a) master painted coil (40,000–48,000 lb,
41.5"–43" wide for 36"-coverage panel) direct from coil-coaters/distributors —
**United Steel Supply** (Austin TX; painted Galvalume specialist supplying
exactly this trade [V]), Steel Dynamics/Steelscape via distributors, or (b)
slit/split coil from service centers — **Kloeckner Cincinnati** (prepaint
slitting, explicitly serves KY/IN [V]), **Kloeckner Memphis** (western KY [V]),
**Steel Warehouse** (South Bend IN [V presence, E terms]), **Majestic Steel**
(prepainted coil program [V]), or from panel-industry redistributors
(**Sheffield Metals**: slit-coil minimum just 100 LF, stock 16/20/22/24"
widths [V — Jan 2024 ordering guide]; **Western States Metal Roofing**:
retail-ish $1.99–2.29/sq ft pre-painted coil [V listing]).

- **Per-color minimums:** the reported "~3–5 ton per color" floor is
  **directionally right for buying *split-to-weight* painted coil from
  distributors [E]** — one verified public datapoint: a supplier minimum of
  **5,000 sq ft per stock color (500 sq ft special-order) [V — search of
  supplier terms, Sep 2026]**; 5,000 sq ft of 29-ga ≈ ~3,000 lb ≈ 1.5 tons,
  and custom-color *paint runs* at a coater are 20,000+ lb [E]. Practical
  stocking for a startup mill: 8–12 colors × 3–5 tons ≈ **$50K–$90K of coil
  working capital [E]**.
- **Painted vs bare [E, anchored on verified benchmarks]:** Nucor HRC spot
  **$1,170/ST (17 Aug 2026) [V, carried over]** → cold-rolled+galv extras →
  bare Galvalume ~$1,550–1,750/ST (~$0.78–0.88/lb) [E, carried over]; paint
  (SMP two-coat) adds ~$0.10–0.18/lb [E] → **painted truckload cost
  ~$0.88–1.05/lb**; small-lot distributor slit coil $1.20–1.60/lb-equivalent
  [E from $1.99–2.29/sq ft retail listings].
- **The secondary/seconds market — the claim checks out [V mechanism]:**
  secondary = coil that failed prime cosmetic/dimensional cert or is excess/
  unallocated inventory; sold at meaningful discounts with full mechanical
  integrity (Collier Metals, Atlanta/Dallas — dedicated excess-and-secondary
  house [V]; Victory Metal Roofing & Supply moves Mill Steel coated seconds
  into exactly this trade [V]). Discount magnitude 15–30% under prime [E,
  consistent with prior file's mill-seconds finding]. **This — cash buying of
  secondary/excess painted coil in truckload lots, plus family labor and no
  debt — is the Amish price advantage. It is a *purchasing* advantage, not a
  machinery advantage,** and Misty Valley can buy the same coil the day it
  owns a machine that can run it.

## 7. The two paybacks

### Conversion: 36"-coverage panel → 1 square (100 sq ft) = **33.3 LF**.

### (a) Panel line

**The spread.** MRS's own published model [V-2018, directionally current]:
sell **$2.50/LF**, direct material **$1.90/LF** → **$0.60/LF gross spread**.
2026 check [E]: 29-ga painted panel takes ~2.0–2.2 lb/LF; coil at
$0.88–1.05/lb → material $1.80–2.30/LF; Dutchman-style same-day mill retail
runs ~$2.50–3.25/LF [E; 29-ga retail comps $1.75–3.00/sq ft [V range] and one
$4.81/LF outlier]. Spread holds at **$0.55–0.90/LF gross**; net of incremental
labor, scrap, packaging: **~$0.40–0.60/LF contribution [E]**. Buying secondary
coil at 15–30% off adds ~$0.30–0.50/LF more — that's the whole Amish model.

**Squares/month for a 3-year machine payback (at $0.50/LF net contribution):**

| Machine | Needs/yr | LF/month | **Squares/month** |
|---|---|---|---|
| $48K new CSC ag machine [V price] | $16K | ~2,700 | **~80** |
| $85K used dual-deck AG+PBR + $20K decoiler ≈ **$105K** [V prices] | $35K | ~5,800 | **~175** |
| **$150K used line (the brief's case)** | $50K | ~8,300 | **~250** |
| **$400K new US line (the brief's case)** | $133K | ~22,200 | **~665** |

At the optimistic $0.90/LF gross spread the $150K case drops to ~140 sq/mo and
the $400K case to ~370 sq/mo; at MRS's plain $0.60/LF they're ~230 and ~615.
**The $400K line is a Dutchman-scale bet** (they and Superior already serve
this corridor same-day); **the $150K-and-under tier pays back at volumes a
single busy supply store can actually generate** — 100–250 squares/month is
8–20 pole barns or 25–60 residential roofs a month through the yard.

### (b) Stud line — gated by certification, carried from `CHINA-STUD-IMPORT.md`

Machinery is trivially cheap ($8K–10K Chinese [V]; $250K+ NA [E]). Material
math [carried, V/E]: domestic light-gauge galv coil ~$0.80/lb → 25-ga EQ
stud ≈ $3.20 material + forming vs. **$5.50–6.50/pc est. domestic wholesale**
→ gross $2–3/stud. A $50K landed line at $2/stud pays back on ~25,000 studs
(≈ 2 truckloads). **But:**

- **The gate [V, prior file §5]:** code-driven commercial work requires a
  current **ICC-ES evaluation report** (AC46/AC86 → AISI S100/S220/S240,
  IBC §2204) per profile family — **6–12+ months of testing and QA audits**
  [V-carried], plus UL/Intertek listing to appear in fire-rated wall designs,
  plus run-specific MTRs (A653 substrate / A1003 member). All-in certification
  cost for one profile family incl. testing, quality manual, audits:
  **est. $50K–$150K and a year [E — prior file verified the timeline, not the
  dollars]**. Until then the salable market is residential remodel/non-rated
  light commercial — under half of corridor stud volume [E-carried].
- **The competition [V-carried]:** ClarkDietrich plants sit 100–250 mi away —
  the shortest mill-to-market leg in the country — and the petitioners' club
  (ClarkDietrich, CEMCO, Telling, Marino) is capitalized and litigious.
- **Real all-in barrier:** $10K machine + $30K slit-coil program + $50–150K
  and 12 months of certification + a UL problem you can't buy your way out of
  quickly = **$100K–$250K and a year before the first legally-spec'able
  commercial stud** — to enter a commodity ClarkDietrich already trucks in
  daily. The prior file's route (b) — **domestic coil through an existing
  regional roll-former at est. $3.70–4.30/pc, their ESR and MTRs included** —
  delivers the same economics with zero of this.

## 8. Verdicts

**Panel line — buy used, later, small; trigger = ~100 squares/month.** Don't
buy a $400K MRS/Bradbury line now: that's a 600+ sq/mo machine and
Dutchman/Superior already give the corridor same-day panels, so Misty Valley
would be entering its own suppliers' business at their strongest point. But
the entry price is far lower than assumed — $48K new (CSC, [V]) to ~$105K
(used dual-deck AG+PBR + decoiler, [V listings]) — and pays back inside 3
years once the yard is *reselling* ~100–175 squares/month of other people's
panels. So: track monthly panel resale volume starting now; at a sustained
100 sq/mo, buy the used dual-deck machine plus a folder, stock 8–10 colors
(~$60–90K coil incl. a secondary-coil program via Collier/Victory-type
sources), and capture the $0.40–0.60/LF that currently goes to Liberty. Until
that trigger: keep buying from Dutchman, and get a written toll/wholesale rate
from Superior Metals to sharpen the benchmark.

**Stud line — no; and the trigger is certification economics, not volume.**
The machine is a rounding error ($8–10K [V]); the ICC-ES/UL/MTR apparatus
(est. $50–150K + 6–12+ months per profile family [V timeline, E cost]) is the
product, and without it the output is legal only for the smallest slice of
demand — while ClarkDietrich sits 200 miles away and a regional roll-former
will toll-form certified product from domestic coil at ~$3.70–4.30/pc [E,
carried]. Revisit only if Misty Valley ever has captive, contracted demand
above ~2 truckloads/month *and* a partner who already holds an ESR.

**The alternative — toll forming (Superior Metals model):** rates are
quote-only industry-wide [E]; the published anchor is Dahlstrom's worked
example of **~$1.00/ft all-in at 100K ft/yr including tooling amortization
[V — Dahlstrom blog]**, and pure forming charges on simple profiles run well
under that once tooling exists; the prior file's stud toll estimate was
**$0.35–0.60/pc [E-carried]**. On panels the equivalent arbitrage is
documented as factory $4.00/sq ft vs. self-run $1.75/sq ft [V — Metal Roofing
Magazine, Aug/Sep 2025]. **First move, this month, costs nothing: ask Superior
Metals for a written toll rate to run Misty Valley-supplied coil** — if they'll
form your coil at ≤$0.35/LF, you get most of the mill margin with zero capex,
and the machine decision waits for real volume data.
