# China Stud Import — Current Landed Cost & Sourcing Reality

**Research date: 5 September 2026.** Companion to `02-trade-compliance-risk.md`
(duty stack, verified 2026-09-04) and `04-unit-economics.md` (container
economics). This file adds: independent re-verification of the stack, real
supplier leads with listed prices, the four-way landed comparison, and a
verdict. Tariffs and freight move weekly — **re-pull everything before wiring
money.**

**One-line answer up front:** the total effective duty on Chinese steel studs
today is **~75.5% ad valorem** (50% §232 + 25% §301 + ~0.47% MPF/HMF, MFN
Free). At that stack a properly nested container still lands at roughly
**$4.00–4.70 per 3-5/8" × 10' drywall-gauge stud** — about $1.50–2.50/stud
below estimated domestic wholesale acquisition. The gross arbitrage is real
(~$16k–27k/container) and **it is not worth taking**: a single AD/CVD petition
— the exact fact pattern Commerce has worked through product-by-product in
2025–26 — retroactively erases three containers' worth of it, and generic
Chinese studs without an ICC-ES report can't legally go into most of the
commercial work the corridor actually builds. **No-go as a business; marginal
even as a one-time trade.**

## 1. The tariff wall, precisely (verified 5 Sep 2026)

### Classification

**HTS 7308.90.95** (alt. 7308.90.30) — "structures and parts of structures…
prepared for use in structures." CBP has ruled on this exact product from
China: [NY N278206](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/N278206) (steel drywall
studs from China → 7308.90.9590) and NY N306468 (studs and track →
7308.90.3000). Do **not** shop 7216 (semi-finished shapes — wrong once
punched/cut) or 9403 (furniture — wrong entirely). Misclassification is 19 USC
1592 penalty exposure.

Every point of it is trade-remedy tariff, not ordinary duty — the cost basis of
this business moves with politics, on no notice, sometimes retroactively.

## 2. FOB China pricing — real listed suppliers (pulled 5 Sep 2026)

Benchmark input: Chinese export galvanized coil is running roughly
**$680–730/tonne FOB** ([Mysteel FOB China assessments](https://www.mysteel.net/daily-prices/6924102-galvanized-steel-export-prices-fob-china) /
[Kaida market note](https://www.tjkdsteel.com/news/navigating-the-currents-china-s-export-galvan-85262777.html)).

### How many studs fit

**Correction to the brief:** a 3-5/8" 20-ga stud does not weigh 2.2 lb/ft.
Published member weights ([SFIA Technical Guide, Feb 2026](https://sfia.memberclicks.net/assets/Library/TechnicalCatalog/SFIA-technical-product-guide_FINAL_20260217.pdf)):

| Member | Common name | lb/ft | lb per 10-ft piece |
|---|---|---|---|
| 362S125-18 | 3-5/8" **25-ga EQ** drywall stud (the shelf product) | 0.40 | 4.0 |
| 362S125-30 | 3-5/8" **20-ga drywall** (30 mil) | 0.66 | 6.6 |
| 362S162-33 | 3-5/8" 20-ga **structural** (33 mil) | 0.89 | 8.9 |

(2.2 lb/ft is a heavy 16-ga 6" structural member — using it understates the
piece count 3×.)

The binding constraint is **the truck, not the box**: a 40HC rates ~62,900 lb
payload (~28.5 t), but road-legal gross of 80,000 lb minus tractor, chassis and
tare leaves **~44,000 lb (~20 t)** without overweight permits — which matches
the brief's 19–20 t working assumption.

| Product | Pieces per road-legal 44,000 lb load |
|---|---:|
| 25-ga EQ (4.0 lb/pc) | **11,000 pcs** (110,000 LF) |
| **20-ga drywall (6.6 lb/pc)** | **~6,660 pcs** (66,600 LF) |
| 20-ga structural (8.9 lb/pc) | ~4,940 pcs |

> **Nesting is everything.** Loose-stacked studs cube out near half those
> counts and the landed cost per piece doubles. Specify nested bundling in the
> PO; verify on the packing list. (`04` §3.)

### Is the China container alive or dead?

**Alive on paper, by a thin and fragile margin.** Best case (25-ga EQ, nested,
genuine FOB): landed **$4.06/pc** vs est. domestic acquisition $5.50–6.50/pc →
**$1.50–2.50/pc advantage ≈ $16,000–27,000 gross per container**, before the
137-day cash cycle (~$45k locked per container, `04` §5), before the code
losses in §5, and before pricing the AD/CVD tail. Worst realistic case (loose
load, $1,000/t FOB): the advantage rounds to zero. And route (b) — domestic
coil through a regional roll-former — reaches approximately the same cost with
none of the exposure.

## 5. Compliance traps — what kills imported studs on real jobs

1. **ICC-ES evaluation report.** Code-driven commercial work specifies members
   with a current evaluation report demonstrating AISI S100/S220/S240
   compliance (ICC-ES AC46/AC86; IBC §2204 via the ESR). Generic Alibaba studs
   have none, and without one the building official and the EOR can reject the
   substitution. It is *achievable* — importers have done it
   ([ESR-5853, King Steel Studs](https://icc-es.org/wp-content/uploads/report-directory/ESR-5853.pdf);
   [ESR-5237, Liga Steel](https://icc-es.org/wp-content/uploads/report-directory/ESR-5237.pdf);
   [ESR-5191, Steel Tech USA](https://icc-es.org/wp-content/uploads/report-directory/ESR-5191.pdf)) —
   but it costs real money and 6–12+ months of testing/QA audits per profile
   family. Until then the salable market is residential remodel and non-rated
   light commercial. Est. well under half of corridor stud volume.
2. **Fire-rated assemblies.** A UL/Intertek listed wall design names its
   components; an unlisted stud in a rated wall voids the rating — a
   life-safety and insurance problem, not a paperwork one (`03`, `05` §C).
3. **Buy America / BABA.** All federally funded infrastructure requires iron
   and steel **melted and poured through coating in the US**
   ([EPA BABA overview](https://www.epa.gov/baba/build-america-buy-america-baba-act-overview);
   [Steptoe on final OMB guidance](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/final-build-america-buy-america-guidance-published.html)).
   Schools, water, DOT, VA, and much state-funded KY work is closed to imported
   studs regardless of price. Read every spec for "domestic steel" before
   committing material.
4. **Mill certs.** Demand run-specific MTRs (ASTM A653 substrate / A1003
   member, coating class, yield), then micrometer-verify base metal on arrival.
   Chinese "20 ga" at 0.45 mm actual is the classic failure.
5. **First-importer customs basics.** Continuous bond ($50,000 min,
   ~$400–700/yr) or single-entry bonds; ISF ("10+2") filed 24 h before vessel
   loading — late filing = liquidated damages up to $5,000 and exam flags;
   licensed broker ~$150–300/entry; you as Importer of Record carry 19 USC 1592
   liability for the supplier's paperwork. If a supplier offers "DDP, tariffs
   included" at a too-good number, that is undervaluation fraud with your name
   on it (`02` §3).
6. **The AD/CVD tail — the company-ender.** No order today, but: temporary
   steel fencing from China went petition → order in 16 months
   ([order, 13 May 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/13/2026-09460/temporary-steel-fencing-from-the-peoples-republic-of-china-antidumping-duty-order-and-countervailing));
   steel grating (HTS 7308.90.70 — the adjacent code) was just continued on
   sunset review (1 Sep 2026); CORE-China rates run ~451% combined with four
   active 2026 circumvention inquiries; Australia already has an order on this
   exact product (light-gauge stud and track from China). US assessment is
   **retrospective**: deposits now, final duties billed 3–4 years later, with
   interest, at rates you don't control (`02` §4). Petitioners here are
   ClarkDietrich, CEMCO, Telling, Marino — concentrated, capitalized,
   litigious, and 200 miles away.

## 6. Verdict

### NO-GO on the China container as a business.

**The decisive number: 75.5% duty leaves only ~$1.50–2.50/stud (~$16k–27k per
container, gross) over domestic wholesale — and a single retroactive AD/CVD
petition at CORE-like rates (~451%) costs ~$60k–80k per already-entered
container, three to four years after the fact.** The expected value of the
arbitrage is negative the moment you price the tail at any realistic petition
probability, and the 2025–26 docket (fencing ordered, grating continued, four
circumvention inquiries, an Australian order on this exact product) says the
probability is not small. Add that the un-ESR'd product can't ship to rated
commercial jobs or any Buy-America work, that $45k/container sits underwater
for ~137 days, and that domestic coil + a roll-former reaches near the same
cost — the container is a levered short position on US trade policy, not a
supply chain.

### The alternative path, in order

1. **Three phone calls this week (free):** ClarkDietrich (West Chester/Vienna
   OH), Telling Industries, and a GMS or L&W branch — written dealer pricing at
   1–3 containers/month equivalent volume. This sets the real benchmark that
   every other option must beat.
2. **Regional roll-former / toll program:** standard 30-mil and EQ-equivalent
   product from domestic coil at est. $3.70–4.30/pc — China-level cost, zero
   import apparatus, MTRs and ESR come with the mill. Ask Telling, MBA,
   Phillips, and the custom shops in the [4specs 05 41 00 directory](https://www.4specs.com/s/05/05-4100.html)
   about seconds, overruns, and off-spec coil programs — mill seconds and
   distributor overruns routinely clear at 15–30% under list (estimate;
   negotiated case by case).
3. **Quote Panel Rey (Monterrey) delivered Elizabethtown:** USMCA §232
   treatment (15–25% on non-US content vs 75% China), truck freight, days not
   weeks, real ESRs, no ISF. This is the honest "import cheap" play in
   September 2026, and it should be priced before any wire goes west.
4. If the China trade is ever taken anyway: **one container maximum**, every
   Gate 1 box in `05` cleared in writing first — binding ruling, CORE scope
   ruling request filed, nested load verified on the packing list, and no
   fixed-price resale until the landed number is actual, not modeled.
