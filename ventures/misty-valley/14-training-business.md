## 1. The regulatory structure, precisely

### What the Outreach Training Program is (and is not)

The OSHA Outreach Training Program is a **voluntary** federal program. OSHA itself does not require anyone to hold a 10- or 30-hour card; states, cities, and employers are the ones who mandate it. OSHA's own words: "OSHA does not require the training. Although some states, cities, and job creators have mandated Outreach Training Program courses as a prerequisite to employment." (OSHA, "The Facts About Obtaining an OSHA Card," osha.gov/training/oshacardfacts — fetched Sept 5, 2026.)

The chain of authority runs:

```
OSHA Directorate of Training and Education (DTE)
  └─ Authorizing Training Organizations (ATOs) — the OTI Education Centers
       └─ Authorized Outreach Trainers (individuals, authorized 4 years at a time)
            └─ Students — receive DOL course completion cards
  └─ OSHA-authorized ONLINE Outreach providers (a separate, short, named list)
```

Key mechanics, all from the **OSHA Outreach Training Program Requirements** (OTP_Requirements_2024.pdf, osha.gov — fetched Sept 5, 2026):

- **Only authorized Outreach trainers** may teach 10-/30-hour classes and obtain student completion cards. "Only OSHA-authorized trainers may teach 10- and 30-hour safety courses and issue OSHA student course completion cards." (osha.gov/training/oshacardfacts, fetched Sept 5, 2026.)
- **Cards**: The trainer submits an Outreach Training Program Report (OTPR) to their ATO **within 30 calendar days** of class completion; the ATO processes the card request within 30 days; the trainer must issue cards **directly to the student** (not the employer, "regardless of who paid for the training") **within 90 calendar days** of course end. (OTP Requirements 2024, §§ "Distribution/Receipt of Student Course Completion Cards.") DOL completion cards have been durable plastic cards since ~2016 **[UNVERIFIED — plastic-card rollout date from provider marketing, not confirmed on osha.gov]**; card issuance itself and the 30/90-day clocks are verified from the 2024 Requirements document.
- **Class limits (in-person/live)**: minimum 3 students; maximum 40; max 7.5 contact hours per day (so OSHA 10 is minimum two days in person, OSHA 30 minimum four). Video-conference (live) delivery is allowed with 7-day advance notice to the ATO and a 20-student cap without a proctor. (OTP Requirements 2024, §§ V.O, V.R.)
- **Asynchronous online is a closed club**: "Outreach trainers may not conduct asynchronous (not live instructor-led) remote classes. **Only OSHA-authorized online Outreach training providers may conduct asynchronous remote Outreach classes.**" (OTP Requirements 2024, § V.R.1.) You cannot become an online provider by becoming a trainer; OSHA authorizes online providers separately and rarely.
- **Records**: the individual trainer personally owns 5-year record retention and "may not rely on internal or external staff, clients, employers, or third parties to prepare, submit, report, retain, or maintain the required training records." (OTP Requirements 2024, § V.P.) This means Misty Valley the company can never be the trainer of record — Joey the person is.

### What a retailer may and may not do — plainly

- **May**: sell/resell seats (enrollment codes, vouchers, checkout referrals) for courses **delivered under an authorized online provider's authorization**. This is an established, at-scale practice — osha.com and oshaeducationcenter.com are exactly this: private retail storefronts in front of an authorized provider's LMS. The training, the exam, the trainer of record, and the DOL card all remain the provider's. 360training runs formal reseller/affiliate programs for this (see §2).
- **May not**: deliver asynchronous online Outreach training itself, issue cards, imply OSHA approval/endorsement, or use the word "certified" (see §6). A reseller's authorization is entirely derivative — Misty Valley would sell under **the provider's** authorization, via contract with the provider, and must follow the provider's (and OSHA's) advertising rules.
- **May, separately**: employ or contract an **authorized Outreach trainer** (Joey, once authorized) who teaches in-person or live-videoconference classes. The authorization belongs to the individual trainer, not the company; the company can market and host the classes, but Joey personally owns records, OTPRs, and card distribution.

## 3. Path B — Joey as an authorized Outreach trainer

### The real requirements (osha.gov/training/outreach/authorized — fetched Sept 5, 2026)

Construction trainer authorization, verbatim structure:

1. **Experience**: "five years of construction safety experience." A degree in OSH, CSP, or CIH substitutes for **two** of the five years. No waivers; the OTIEC where you train rules on whether your experience qualifies. → **Joey's case**: years of field roofing plus chief-estimator duty likely qualifies, but "construction *safety* experience" is the operative phrase — he should frame his history around safety responsibilities (toolbox talks, fall-protection planning, site safety oversight, OSHA compliance in estimates/means-and-methods) and **get a ruling from EKU's OTIEC before registering**. **[Whether EKU counts his specific history: UNVERIFIABLE in advance — it's their call.]**
2. **OSHA #510** — Occupational Safety and Health Standards for the Construction Industry (the standards course). Prerequisite to #500.
3. **OSHA #500** — Trainer Course in OSHA Standards for Construction. Completing it yields the "Authorized Construction Trainer Card."
4. **Renewal**: authorization lasts **4 years**; renew via **OSHA #502** (Update for Construction Industry Outreach Trainers) before expiration. Let it lapse and you retake #500.

### Delivery economics, in-person OSHA 10, 20-person crew

Call it **$2,200–2,400 net per class**. Two classes a month is a ~$55k/yr side business at near-zero capex.
- **The strategic value is bigger than the fee**: every class is 20 construction workers and a safety manager in a room with Misty Valley for two days. Harness inspections during the fall-protection module, PPE fit checks, "class attendee" discount codes, and the GC relationship that follows — the trainer relationship IS the sales channel. This is the real reason Path B exists: it is customer acquisition that pays for itself.

## 6. Compliance red lines

From the OTP Requirements 2024 advertising section (§ V.Q — fetched Sept 5, 2026), these are enforceable against trainers and, contractually, against resellers:

1. **"OSHA certified" does not exist.** "The OSHA Outreach Training Program is not a certification program… Advertisements must not use any form of the word 'certify,' 'license,' or imply that the… class will result in the individual being certified/licensed." Say: *"OSHA 10-Hour Outreach course — DOL course completion card"* and *"taught by an OSHA-authorized Outreach trainer."* Never: "OSHA certified," "OSHA certification," "get OSHA licensed."
2. **No implied endorsement.** "OSHA" and "Department of Labor" may be used, but never in a way implying the class "is being conducted by, has been approved by, is endorsed by, or is being done in cooperation with OSHA." OSHA does not "approve" trainers or courses.
3. **No DOL/OSHA logos, ever.** "The use of the OSHA logo, the U.S. Department of Labor logo, and the U.S. Department of Labor seal is strictly prohibited."
4. **No card imagery.** No legible depictions of completion cards on the site or in marketing (§ V.Q.3.f). No lookalike "class certificates" that could be confused with DOL cards (§ "Class Certificates").
5. **No "train-the-trainer" or compliance claims.** Can't market Outreach classes as trainer courses; can't state the training "is OSHA compliant or… meets the training requirements of any OSHA standard" (§ V.Q.3.g).
6. **Cards go to students, not employers.** Even when MVS's customer (the contractor) pays, the trainer issues cards directly to workers within 90 days (§ "Distribution of Student Course Completion Cards"). Don't promise employers physical custody of cards — promise roster *status*, which is what the site tracks.
7. **Fraud is criminally prosecuted, and enforcement is real**: an OSHA-authorized NJ trainer pleaded guilty to selling 100+ false OSHA 10 cards at ~$200/card (justice.gov/usao-nj; gbca.com — via search Sept 5, 2026); a Queens trainer got 2 years federal for issuing cards without training (justice.gov SDNY, Dec 2012); a Brooklyn man got jail in 2025 for fake OSHA/DOB cards (brooklyneagle.com, May 2025). OSHA runs a public **Outreach Trainer Watch List** (osha.gov/training/outreach/trainers/watchlist) — Watch List trainers can't even assist at classes; referrals go to DOL-OIG. The lesson for MVS: never touch a card, never "help" a customer who missed hours, never let sales pressure bend attendance rules. One shortcut ends Joey's authorization (4-year asset) and the company's credibility (permanent asset).
8. **State-plan nuance**: KY OSH, TOSHA, and IOSHA accept federal Outreach cards in practice (there's no separate KY card program found), but any *statutory claim* MVS makes on a jurisdictional landing page ("required in Nevada," "required on Missouri public works") must cite the statute and be reviewed before publish — the table in §4 is the starting point, not legal advice. **[Have counsel or the state consultation program sanity-check jurisdiction pages before launch.]**
