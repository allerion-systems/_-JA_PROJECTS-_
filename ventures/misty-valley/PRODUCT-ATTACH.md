# What Else Rides on the Truck

**The question:** is there something more lucrative or easier to sell than metal
studs, or something to sell alongside them?

**The answer is in the framing of the question.** 5 September 2026.

---

## 1 · Attach is not diversification, and only one of them is cheap

| | **ATTACH** | **DIVERSIFY** |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | the one you already have | a new one |
| Takeoff | the one you already did | a new skill |
| Truck | the one already going | a second run |
| Supplier | often the same one | a new relationship, new credit, new terms |
| Inventory | marginal | a second position |
| Incremental cost | **near zero** | **a second company** |

> **The test for every product below: does it appear on the same wall section,
> on the same set of drawings, bought by the same person, delivered on the same
> truck?** If yes, it is nearly free revenue. If no, it is a second business
> wearing a product's clothes.

**And the constitution already answers this.** Core Focus: *cut, labeled,
sequenced framing packages, delivered by floor and by phase.* Article VII puts
"whether we stay in steel alone" firmly in the **changeable** column — so
expanding is allowed. But EOS's discipline is that anything outside the niche
gets a no **even when it is profitable.** Attach stays inside the niche.
Diversifying does not.

## 2 · The attach list, ranked

*Ranked by how tightly it binds to a takeoff Joey is already doing.*

### Tier 1 — do these, they are on the drawings already

**1. CFS accessories — clips, bridging, deflection and slotted track, backing
plates, header and jamb components.**
Spec'd on the same wall schedule, quantities fall straight out of the same
takeoff, ships with the studs. ClarkDietrich, Simpson Strong-Tie, Steel Network.
**A stud package sold without its clips and bridging is an incomplete package —
and the crew buys the missing half from somebody else at retail.** This is not an
expansion, it is finishing the product.

**2. Fasteners — screws, anchors, powder-actuated.**
Fastenal's own 10-K says non-fastener products carry **lower** gross margin than
its overall business — **fasteners are the margin engine, not the volume
engine.** Negligible freight, negligible cube, and the crew is buying them
anyway. The cheapest attach on this list.

**3. Firestopping — head-of-wall joints, penetrations, listed systems.**
STI, Hilti, 3M. Small dollars, high margin, and **structurally spec-locked**: when
a spec names a UL design, "or approved equal" is legally hollow because an equal
must carry its own listing for that assembly and often none exists. This is the
same wall Joey already took off, and it is the item most often forgotten until
the inspector is standing there. **Highest-margin attach on the list.**

### Tier 2 — real, but they change something

**4. Exterior sheathing and weather barrier** — for exterior CFS scope. Bulky
and freight-hungry, but it is on the same wall.

**5. Access doors and panels** — small, high margin, always missed in the
buyout. Div 8, but sold to the same person.

**6. Corner bead, trim, control joints** — attaches to the drywall, not the
studs. One step further out.

### Tier 3 — say no for now

**7. Drywall itself.** Heavy, freight-expensive, low margin, and the channel just
consolidated hard: **Home Depot bought SRS Distribution for $18.25B and then
GMS** — 1,340+ locations, 4,000+ trucks, explicitly aimed at jobsite delivery.
GMS runs 31.2% blended gross margin at national scale. **Do not walk into that
fight to carry board at a loss to be "full service."**

**8. Insulation.** All cube, no dollars. Freight eats it.

## 3 · The one genuinely bigger play — and it is a second business

**Division 8 openings: hollow metal frames, doors, hardware, and Division 28
access-control coordination.**

The research in `../adjacent-plays/where-the-margin-actually-is.md` found this is
the **only** high-value spec-locked category a new entrant can actually enter —
electrical, mechanical, fire pumps and dampers are all closed behind rep
territories and factory dealer agreements you will not be granted.

Why it is attractive:

- **Lines are obtainable.** Hollow metal and hardware distribution is fragmented
  and regional.
- **The margin is in labor, not inventory** — hardware scheduling, keying
  schedules, submittal packages, frame elevations. Low working capital.
- **Geography is on your side.** Allegion's own shipping table puts Republic's
  plant in **Tennessee — 1–2 day transit** to the whole corridor.
- **The failure mode is documented and almost nobody reads it.** Per Allegion's
  guide dated 1 September 2026: Schlage locksets **3 weeks**, LCN closers **2
  weeks**, Ives Fast Track **3–5 days** — and **all mag locks 16–20 weeks**, in
  the same hardware set. Shop drawings **9–10 weeks** after order.

**Why it is not an attach:** different takeoff skill, different buyout timing,
different supplier network, and the margin needs a **DHI-credentialed hardware
consultant (AHC)** — a hiring problem, not a purchasing problem.

> **Verdict: this is Phase 3, not Phase 1.** Put it on the ten-year plan, not on
> the truck. Opening a second business before the first has an outside customer
> is how three people end up with two half-companies.

## 4 · What I do not know, and neither does anyone else

**There is no public, audited dataset of distributor gross margin by product
category.** Not for PPE, not for fasteners, not for firestopping, not for
partitions. I looked. Anyone who quotes you "toilet partitions are a 32% margin
category" is quoting an anecdote.

**The rankings above are structural** — channel access, freight-per-dollar, spec
lock, and attachment to an existing takeoff — **not margin data.** Before
committing inventory to any of them, do the cheap thing: **get three real quotes
and three real sell prices on one job.** One afternoon of phone calls beats every
industry report.

---

## 5 · The blunt answer

**Nothing on this list is more lucrative than the takeoff.**

Three independent research threads this week landed on the same conclusion:
**the money is in reading the spec before the buyout, not in the SKU.** Adding
products does not change the position — it just gives you more to sell from the
same position.

> **So attach Tier 1 now, because it is free.
> Do not diversify until Supply has twenty-five outside customers and a person
> in the Operations seat who is not a partner.**

That is the FY2027 plan already. Adding a product line before it is finished is
the most common way a good distributor dies with a full warehouse.
