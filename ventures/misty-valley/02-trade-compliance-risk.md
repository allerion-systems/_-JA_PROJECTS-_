## 1. Classification — settled, and it matters

**HTS 7308.90.9590** (or 7308.90.3000). CBP has ruled on this exact product from
this exact country:

| Ruling | Product | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| [NY N278206](https://rulings.cbp.gov/ruling/N278206) | Steel drywall studs **from China** — light-gauge cold-formed galvanized, machine formed/cut/punched, C-shaped with cutouts | **7308.90.9590** |
| [NY N306468](https://www.customsmobile.com/rulings/docview?doc_id=NY+N306468) | Cold-formed galvanized drywall **studs and track**, non-load-bearing partitions | **7308.90.3000** |

Heading 7308 covers steel *"prepared for use in structures."* A punched,
cut-to-length C-stud is the textbook case.

**Don't shop for a better code.** 7216.91 is the *semi-finished* provision and
is wrong once the section is punched and cut. 7228.70 is worse — it carries an
extra 25% under the Section 301 four-year review that 7308.90 does *not*.
Misclassification is a 19 USC 1592 penalty exposure, not a savings strategy.

> **Action:** get a **binding ruling** in Misty Valley's own name under 19 CFR
> Part 177 before the first entry. It's free, takes 30–90 days, and the two
> rulings above make it straightforward.

### **Total: 75% ad valorem. Landed = FOB × 1.7547 + freight + port + inland.**

The entire duty burden is **trade-remedy tariffs, not ordinary customs duty**.
The MFN rate is Free. That means the whole cost structure of this business moves
with *politics*, not with trade agreements — which is the argument in `06`.

### Two things that changed and that most people still have wrong

**The "steel content" rule is repealed.** U.S. Note 16(c) now reads that the
headings *"apply to the **full customs value**"*, and the 15%-by-weight metal
threshold applies **only to articles NOT in chapters 72, 73, 74 or 76.** Steel
studs are Chapter 73. **No apportionment, no metal-content test, no split
rate — 50% of the entire invoice.** Anyone still modelling on a steel-content
basis is understating the duty. (Proclamation 11021, 2 April 2026, effective
6 April 2026 — [91 FR 18201](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/09/2026-06960/strengthening-actions-taken-to-adjust-imports-of-aluminum-steel-and-copper-into-the-united-states).)

**The IEEPA tariffs are gone — that's the good news, and it isn't enough.**
The Supreme Court struck them down 6–3 in *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump*
on 20 February 2026; CBP stopped collecting on 24 February. Section 122 was
invoked, held unlawful by the CIT on 7 May 2026, and expired 23 July 2026.

> **Money on the table:** if Misty Valley Contracting or any affiliate imported
> **anything** from China between February 2025 and February 2026, they may be
> owed refunds through CBP's **CAPE portal**. Over 75,000 claims have been
> filed. **Ask the broker.** There may be a second claim for Section 122 duties
> paid Feb–Jul 2026 — refund posture there is unsettled; get counsel.

**Watch for the replacement.** The administration retains Section 232 expansion,
Section 338, and broader 301 authority. Note also that the BIS
three-windows-per-year derivative inclusions process was **terminated** on
6 April 2026 — Commerce and USTR now add derivative articles **on a rolling
basis** with no predictable calendar. 7308.90 is already fully covered, so for
Ben this is downside-only.

## 3. What this does to the $11,000

```
Claimed all-in landed                             $11,000
Ocean freight alone (Drewry, 2026-09-03)        −  $9,587
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Left for goods + 75% duty + fees + inland dray   $ 1,413
```

Back-solving at the verified stack: the FOB invoice would have to be **$788 per
container** — about **$39/tonne** for 20 tonnes of galvanized, roll-formed,
punched, cut-to-length steel. Chinese *scrap* is $300–350/tonne. See `04`.

**The only way to actually hit $11,000 is to understate the invoice.** That is
customs fraud, and under 19 USC 1592 the importer of record carries it — not
the Chinese supplier.

> **🚩 If a supplier offers "DDP $11,000" or "we handle the customs" — walk
> away.** At a verified 75% stack no legitimate operator delivers that price.
> That offer is the signature of undervaluation, false origin declaration, or
> misclassification, and the liability lands on Misty Valley.

## 4. AD/CVD — the risk that ends companies

**No order currently reaches cold-formed steel framing from China.** A Federal
Register search of ITA notices from January 2025 forward found no order or
petition on steel framing, metal studs, or drywall profiles from any country.
The CORE (corrosion-resistant steel) China order does **not** reach roll-formed
studs — its scope is confined to **flat-rolled** products and its HTS list
contains no heading 7308.

**Absence of an order today is not safety.** Five reasons:

1. **The written scope description is dispositive, not the HTS list.** Commerce
   is not bound by CBP's classification.
2. **CORE's scope covers products "worked after rolling"** with non-rectangular
   cross-sections, and expressly says **cutting and punching do not remove
   merchandise from scope.** Roll-forming isn't listed either way. The line
   between "cut and punched coil" (in scope) and "roll-formed section" (out) is
   the entire ballgame.
3. **CORE China rates are AD 209.97% + CVD 241.07% ≈ 451%.**
4. **Four active CORE circumvention inquiries opened in 2026** — China/Indonesia
   and Vietnam (March), Korea (April), and **China/Thailand (July, brought by
   Nucor and Steel Dynamics)**. Sourcing "Vietnamese" or "Thai" studs
   roll-formed from Chinese substrate lands inside an active enforcement
   program, retroactively. **Do not try to solve the 75% by transshipping.**
5. **🔴 Australia already has an order on this exact product** — Anti-Dumping
   Commission Case 679, *Light Gauge Steel Stud and Track from China*. Foreign
   orders are a well-established leading indicator for US petitions.

**The trend line is unmistakable.** Commerce and the ITC are working through
downstream fabricated Chinese steel articles one product at a time:

| Product | Status |
|---|---|
| **Temporary steel fencing from China** | Petition 2025-01-14 → **ORDER 2026-05-13** (~16 months) |
| **Steel grating from China** — HTS **7308.90.70**, the code next to ours | Order **continued** 2026-09-01 on third sunset review |
| Steel racks and parts from China | Active 2026 |
| CORE from China | In force; two new circumvention inquiries in 2026 |

The US framing industry — ClarkDietrich, MarinoWARE, CEMCO, Telling — is
concentrated, well-capitalized, and litigious. A petition filed the month Ben
scales up puts **every container already on the water** at risk.

### Why it's retroactive — the mechanism nobody expects

The US uses a **retrospective** assessment system. Per
[19 CFR 351.212](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-III/part-351/subpart-B/section-351.212):
*"final liability for antidumping and countervailing duties is determined
**after** merchandise is imported."*

1. What you pay at entry is a **cash deposit**, not the duty.
2. Liquidation is **suspended indefinitely** — no clock runs in your favor.
3. Any interested party — **including the domestic petitioner** — can request an
   annual administrative review. You don't control this.
4. If your Chinese supplier doesn't cooperate or isn't individually examined,
   **you inherit the China-wide rate via adverse facts available.** You have no
   control over the exporter's cooperation and no practical remedy against it.
5. CBP bills the difference **with interest from the date of entry**, typically
   **three to four years later.**
6. **Trap:** fail to file a statement of non-reimbursement before liquidation
   and **CBP presumes reimbursement and doubles the duties.**

```
Year 0    Container enters. Deposit $0.
Year 1-2  Petition → preliminary determination → suspension.
          "Critical circumstances" can reach back 90 days FURTHER.
Year 2-3  Petitioner requests administrative review.
Year 3-4  Supplier doesn't respond → adverse facts available → 451% assessed.
Year 4    CBP bill arrives. Plus interest. On material installed years ago.
```

On a $16,000 container at CORE rates that's **~$72,000 of AD/CVD** on top of
$12,000 already paid — against a business whose whole budget for the container
was $11,000. **There is no "we already spent it" defense.** The importer of
record is liable; the surety pays CBP and then comes after whoever signed the
indemnity.

**Plus EAPA.** Under [19 USC 1517](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-165),
a competitor — say a domestic framing manufacturer who dislikes your pricing —
can allege evasion. Within **90 days** CBP takes interim measures on mere
*"reasonable suspicion"*: suspending liquidation and demanding full-rate cash
deposits on every entry, for months, **before any finding of wrongdoing.** Your
working capital is gone while you're still innocent.

### The mitigation that actually exists

**File a scope ruling request under [19 CFR 351.225](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-III/part-351/subpart-B/section-351.225)**
asking Commerce to confirm that roll-formed studs and track fall **outside** the
CORE China orders. That converts an open-ended contingent liability into a
documented determination. **Do it before volume builds, not after.**

## 5. Bonds and fees

**Continuous bond:** greater of **$50,000** or 10% of duties/taxes/fees in the
prior 12 months, in $10,000 increments (CBP, *How CBP Sets Bond Amounts*, Feb
2024). At ~$12,000/container in duty, a $50,000 bond covers roughly 4
containers/year. Budget **$400–700/year** plus **$150–250 per entry** in
brokerage.

**If AD/CVD ever attaches**, CBP may demand an additional single transaction
bond *"reasonably tailored to the evidence of additional risk"* — no fixed
formula. Sureties typically require **cash collateral or an LC for 100% of the
penal sum** from AD/CVD-exposed importers with no track record. Some decline
outright. On a $16,000 container at CORE rates that's roughly **$100,000 in
cash to move one box.**

**FY2027 user fees take effect 1 October 2026** — under a month out. FY2026 saw
a 34.3% jump. Re-verify the MPF min/max for anything landing in Q4.

## 7. The checklist

Before wiring anything:

- [ ] Engage a **licensed customs broker**. Not optional, not later.
- [ ] Get the **HTS classification in writing** from the broker.
- [ ] Ask, in these words: *"Are there any AD/CVD orders, scope rulings, or
      pending petitions that could cover this product from China?"* Keep the
      written answer.
- [ ] **File a binding ruling request** (19 CFR Part 177).
- [ ] **File a scope ruling request** on CORE (19 CFR 351.225).
- [ ] Decide **DDP vs. IOR** (`01` §5). If IOR, have a customs attorney read the
      surety's **General Indemnity Agreement** before Ben signs.
- [ ] **File ISF 24 hours before vessel loading.** Late filing carries
      per-violation liquidated damages and flags you for examination.
- [ ] Check whether any affiliate is owed **IEEPA refunds** via CAPE.
- [ ] Re-verify every rate against CBP CSMS and the current HTS revision.

Set a standing reminder to re-check AD/CVD petition activity **before every
order**, not once. The whole risk profile of this business can change with a
single filing you didn't see.
