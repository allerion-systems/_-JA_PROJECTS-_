# The Clean Exit & The First Hundred Customers

*Directive: leave R&B honorably, set up so the leaving is clean, and build Misty
Valley's buyer base so no single customer — R&B included — is the company's oxygen.*

## 1 · The clean exit from R&B (between now and October)

**The standard: leave the way you'd want your best estimator to leave you.**

1. **Disclosure first** (instrument drafted, attorney reviewing) — signed before the
   first MVS quote reaches Scott's desk. Done in daylight, it converts "side deal"
   into "vendor file."
2. **Full effort to the last day.** Your R&B work doesn't slip — the estimates you
   owe them get your best until the badge comes off. That's the fact a jury, a
   reference call, and your own mirror all weigh the same way.
3. **Real notice, real transition.** Two weeks minimum (more if a bid cycle is
   mid-flight), a written handoff of open estimates, and an offer to train whoever
   inherits the desk. The transition memo goes in your personal file — it is also
   evidence of good faith.
4. **What leaves with you: nothing of theirs.** No estimate files, no customer lists,
   no supplier pricing, no templates built on their time. Skills and reputation are
   yours; documents are theirs. (Anything MVS/Allerion was built on your own time and
   equipment — keep the receipts that show it.)
5. **No recruiting from R&B** — not their people, and their customers only if those
   customers come to MVS on its public merits, never through R&B information.
6. **The last conversation:** thank Scott plainly, tell him MVS will always quote R&B
   fair, and leave the door open. The roofing world in Kentucky is small — the exit
   IS marketing.

## 2 · Why R&B must be a customer, never the customer

Pre-mortem math: a supply company with one buyer is that buyer's captive — one
dispute from zero. Rule: **no customer over ~30% of revenue by end of year one.**
The 455-square order is a launch, not a business model.

## 3 · First-customer motions (Ben's ground game, starting now)

**The wedge products that open doors:** IMP panels in six cores (few stock them
locally), steel framing delivered to the jobsite, fall-protection with the
compliance answer attached, and Elijah's premium portables.

1. **Jobsite visits (the oldest play, still the best).** Ben drives the I-65 corridor
   — Elizabethtown, Glasgow, Bowling Green — with the line card and a box of donuts.
   Every roofing crew, framing crew, and pole-barn builder gets: "One call, one
   invoice, jobsite delivery. Quotes back in 48 hours." Ten visits a week; the deal
   files start themselves.
2. **The community network.** Ben's people build for a living. The Amish and
   ex-Amish building community around Hart County runs on word of mouth and fair
   dealing — exactly the reputation game MVS is built to win. One fair deal there
   multiplies.
3. **Facebook Marketplace — the proven channel.** Elijah already sells units there.
   MVS lists the premium portables and STR units (our own photos of his actual
   builds — the weekend photo shoot), each listing pointing to the designer link so
   buyers configure and get honest line-item pricing no Marketplace seller shows.
4. **Local GCs and pole-barn builders** bidding schools/ag buildings: the
   sealed-drawings service plus steel package is a subcontract-shaped offer.
5. **County and micro-purchase lane** (once Allerion's SAM.gov activates): sheds
   delivered-and-set, conex boxes, dock hardware — the GOVCON-SPECS file has the
   exact solicitations to answer.
6. **Farmers and lake owners:** purlins/panels for ag repair, dock sections for
   Nolin and Green River — seasonal, local, cash-friendly.
7. **Every quote, win or lose, enters the follow-up file.** A no today is a customer
   whose supplier will eventually miss a delivery.

**The 90-day scoreboard (honest numbers, reviewed monthly):**
- 40 quote requests · 12 paying accounts · no account >30% of revenue
- 2 Marketplace unit sales (Elijah class) · 1 government micro-purchase bid submitted
- Every deal in a numbered file per the evidence rules.

## 4 · What "with divinity" means operationally
Sell only what we'd stand behind in person; label honestly (the structure-not-a-
dwelling line, the representative-image line); quote fair and identical for friend
and stranger; pay suppliers on their terms; disclose what a customer would want to
know before they'd want to ask it. That conduct is the moat no competitor can buy.
