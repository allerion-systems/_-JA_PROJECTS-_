# Where the Margin Actually Is

Prepared for **Joey Allee**, 5 September 2026.

> The ask was: *"Find the hardest to source and most specified products sold to
> construction companies for job sites"* — and separately, *"find the most
> bought items at construction sites"* for an online store and vending program.

Those are opposite businesses. This document separates them, tells you which one
your skill is worth anything in, and leads with the finding that outranks both.

---

## 0. The finding that outranks everything else

**East South Central — Kentucky, Tennessee, Alabama, Mississippi —
nonresidential building starts are down 16.3% year to date through May 2026.
The national total is up 17.6%.** East North Central is +187.8%. South Atlantic
is +44.6%.
([ConstructConnect, 30 Jun 2026](https://news.constructconnect.com/regional-us-construction-activity-reveals-two-speed-nonresidential-market))

Skanska's Summer 2026 read agrees on the mix: capital is going into data
centers, energy, advanced manufacturing and pharma, while **office, retail,
higher education and general commercial remain weak** — which is exactly the
segment mix any of these plays would serve.

> 🔴 **Both business models below are being proposed into a regional market
> contracting 16% while the national market expands 18%.**

That does not kill either idea. It does decide between them. A **volume- and
logistics-dependent business is structurally worse in a shrinking regional
market**, because its economics are density and throughput. A
**knowledge-dependent business can run at lower volume and can follow work out
of the region.** Hold that thought until §5.

## 1. The hardest-to-source products — and the number that proves it

The best evidence is not a lead-time blog. It is the Producer Price Index,
because it measures pricing power directly and the methodology is stable.

| Category (NAICS) | Jan 2020 → Jul 2026 | YoY |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Switchgear / switchboard / panelboard (335313)** | 217.2 → 394.8 = **+81.8%** | **+8.55%** |
| HVAC equipment (333415) | 204.2 → 324.9 = +59.1% | +4.4% |
| Bolt / nut / screw / rivet / washer (332722) | 201.1 → 328.8 = +63.5% | +2.8% (6 mo.) |

[PCU335313335313](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PCU335313335313) ·
[PCU333415333415](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PCU333415333415) ·
[PCU332722332722](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PCU332722332722)

**Electrical distribution gear has roughly double the pricing power of
mechanical equipment and triple that of fasteners.** That is the whole
"spec-locked and supply-constrained" thesis in one line.

Corroborated from the other side: **Eaton's electrical backlog is up 103%
(54% organic)** while commercial/institutional grows only "low teens"
([Eaton Q1 2026](https://www.eaton.com/us/en-us/company/news-insights/news-releases/2026/eaton-reports-record-first-quarter-2026-results.html)).
Read that carefully — the capacity is going to data centers, and a medical
office building is competing for residual factory slots on the same line.

### ⚠️ Two corrections you need before you quote any of this

**1. The headline lead times are for equipment you will never buy.** 160-week
substation transformers, 143-week GSU transformers, 90–110-week 3–4 MW gensets
— that is utility and hyperscale equipment. A 4,000 SF QSR or a 12,000 SF
medical office buys a 400–2000A switchboard, branch panelboards, a 75–500 kVA
dry-type transformer, maybe a 100–500 kW genset. Dry-types in that range run
6–12 weeks standard with real distributor stock. **Anyone selling you a plan
built on "160-week transformer lead times" for light commercial is confused or
selling you something.**

**2. The most-cited public lead-time index appears to be republishing 2022
data.** The VAWN Electrical Equipment Lead Time Index presents itself as
updated 13 Aug 2026 with a credible methodology. Its LV switchgear /
switchboard / panelboard / busduct figures are **identical, value for value**,
to a Siemens CompAS page whose companion bulletin is
[dated 31 May 2022](https://sandy.compas.siemens-info.com/leadtime-adjustments-on-switchboards-and-panelboards_4830.html).
**Treat those week counts as four years stale.** Get the week count from a rep
quote on a live job, not from the internet.

**Also note:** Trane's backlog is a record **$12.1B, up 70%**, Americas
commercial HVAC up ~90%, applied bookings +130%, at a **~9-month order-to-ship**
— and the HVAC PPI is only +4.4%. **Backlog is not pricing power. The PPI is
the tell.** Trane has expanded applied capacity 4× in three years and is not
extracting price. Mechanical is a worse play than electrical despite looking
the same on backlog.

## 2. Who controls the supply — the part that decides everything

| Category | Channel | Can a new entrant get a line? |
|---|---|---|
| Switchgear, switchboards, panelboards | Authorized distributor + rep | **No** |
| Generators / ATS | **Factory dealer, territory-exclusive** (Cat, Cummins) | **No** |
| Chillers / AHU / VRF | Factory-direct offices + rep agencies | **No** |
| Fire pumps, fire/smoke dampers | **Rep agency** — the classic rep category | **No** |
| **Hollow metal, frames, door hardware** | **Distributor with a hardware shop** | **✅ Yes** |
| **Division 10 specialties** | Distributor / specialty contractor, fragmented | **✅ Yes** |
| Access control hardware (Div 28) | Certified integrator | Partially |

**The rep-dominated categories have no inventory position to take and no
product to resell.** The rep is paid a commission to get the product into the
spec; the goods ship factory-direct. **You cannot buy in — you must be
appointed to a territory, and territories are assigned.** And NEMRA's 2026
advocacy is about *declining* commission rates on reps absorbing more work
([EdisonReport](https://edisonreport.com/2026/03/04/the-cost-of-representation-nemra-reps-call-for-better-pos-reporting/)).
That is a market whose economics are getting worse for the incumbents. Bad door
to try to force.

**Electrical is the hardest no.** US electrical distribution is $145.3B with
the **top ten holding ~52%** and **60+ acquisitions in five years**; Sonepar
alone bought eight large independents in two years
([EC&M Top 50](https://www.ecmweb.com/product-sourcing-supply/article/55131107/2024s-top-50-electrical-distributors)).
Louisville and Nashville are already covered by Rexel, CED, Winsupply, Wiseway.
**In a supply-constrained market manufacturers do not add distributors — they
ration the ones they have.** Eaton, with backlog up 103%, has no reason on
earth to appoint a new Kentucky gear distributor.

> **Any plan that requires a franchise appointment in a supply-constrained
> category is not a plan.**

## 3. The one category with current primary data — and it inverts the story

**Republic Doors & Frames (Allegion) publishes a weekly lead-time bulletin
against a stated 98%+ on-time standard. Current week, dated 3 September 2026 —
two days ago:**

- Frames: stock 1 wk · KD 3-sided **6 wks** · welded **7 wks** · specialty
  (lead-lined, ballistic) 8–9 wks
- Doors: stock 1 wk · non-stock DP **3 wks** · DL/DE **6 wks** ·
  steel-stiffened/hurricane **7 wks** · specialty (bullet, lead-lined, STC>43)
  10–11 wks

([Republic bulletin, PDF](https://www.republicdoor.com/content/dam/republic/literature/republic-lead-times/Republic_Lead_Time.pdf))

**Allegion's Product Lead-Times Reference Guide, updated 1 September 2026** —
mechanical hardware is *fast*: Schlage ND/L cylindrical and mortise **3 weeks**
standard keying; Von Duprin exits **3–5 weeks**; LCN closers **2 weeks** most
series; Ives/Zero/Glynn-Johnson Fast Track **3–5 days**.

**And then, in the same document:**

| Item | Lead time |
|---|---:|
| **All mag locks and accessories** | **16–20 weeks** |
| Schlage 8200 consoles | 16 wks |
| ANT-400 antenna series | 12 wks |
| Any "Specials" order | 12 wks |
| Schlage MT15 readers | 8 wks |
| **Buy America (Y code) orders** | **lock lead time + 5 weeks** |
| Schlage C-Rose 643E finish | **20 weeks** |
| Ives 484 privacy guard Z-643E/716 | 15 weeks |
| Non-Fast-Track hinges US3/US4/US10 | 9 weeks |
| **Shop drawings after order received** (Unicel) | **9–10 weeks** |

([Allegion guide, PDF](https://us.allegion.com/content/dam/allegion-us-2/portal/secured/product-lead-time-freight/project-lead-times/Commercial_Residential_Products_and_Product_Lead_Time_Reference_Guide.pdf))

> **Doors are not a long-lead category. The delay is manufactured downstream —
> in the submittal cycle, in finish selection, and in the electrified subset.**
>
> **One opening can contain a 3-week lockset, a 5-day hinge, and a 16–20-week
> mag lock, all on the same hardware set.** That mismatch inside a single
> opening is where doors kill schedules, and it is invisible to anyone who
> hasn't read the hardware schedule line by line.

Independent corroboration of the pattern: custom curtainwall shop-drawing
approval routinely runs **60–90 days** while aluminum extrusions are 4–8 weeks.
Two unrelated categories, same structure — **the approval cycle is longer and
more variable than the factory.**

### And the truest answer to "or approved equal"

When a spec names a UL fire-resistance design or an ICC-ES report, "or approved
equal" is legally hollow. National Gypsum's own guidance:

> *"The design number alone is not a complete specification, and a listed
> assembly is not a generic recipe that permits substitutions."*

Some designs are outright proprietary to one manufacturer's products — UL V438
is proprietary to Gold Bond.
([National Gypsum](https://www.nationalgypsum.com/design-resource-center/faq/faq-ul-fire-resistance-directories))
**Firestopping, head-of-wall joints, smoke dampers, fire-rated glazing and
rated door assemblies are sole-sourced by the listing, not by the architect.**
That is structural pricing power, not cyclical — and it is the same mechanism
`03-product-compliance-risk.md` flagged from the liability side.

## 4. The consumables side — and why it is closed by capital

**First, an honest limit: jobsite transaction counts and dollar rankings by
category do not exist publicly.** No trade association, no BLS series, no public
company discloses it. Anyone who sells you that number is selling you an
anecdote. What *is* knowable is the economics of the businesses that do it.

| | Gross margin | Inventory turns | Basis |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Fastenal** | **45.0%** | ~2.7× (derived) | Audited FY2025 |
| **Grainger** | **39.1%** | ~4.8× (derived) | Audited FY2025 |
| MSC Industrial | ~41% | 3.4× (disclosed) | FY2025 |
| Electrical distribution | ~22% gross, **~3% net** | — | NAED PAR, dated |

Fastenal FY2025: net sales **$8,200.5M**, fasteners 30.5% of sales, **safety
supplies 22.2%**, 1,595 branches, **~124,000 vending devices**, managed
inventory **44.7% of sales**. And the number that matters most here:
**non-residential construction is 8.1% of Q4 sales.** Manufacturing is 75.4%.
Fastenal's own 10-K says **non-fastener products carry lower gross margin than
the overall business** — so PPE and abrasives sit *below* 45% and fasteners sit
*above*. **Fasteners are the margin engine; safety is the volume engine.**
([Fastenal 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/815556/000081555626000009/fast-20251231.htm))

**Who you would be fighting:**

- **Home Depot** — **$18.25B** for SRS Distribution, plus GMS in 2025. Now
  2,000+ US stores, 1,340+ SRS locations, **4,000+ trucks**, 2,500+ outside
  reps, explicitly targeting jobsite delivery.
  ([Home Depot](https://corporate.homedepot.com/news/company/home-depot-announces-agreement-acquire-srs-distribution-leading-specialty-trade))
- **White Cap** — **>$4B**, 133+ branches, CD&R/Sterling-backed and actively
  rolling up.

> This category is a **logistics and working-capital business at 2.7–4.8
> turns**, defended by branch density and truck fleets, and it is the explicit
> target of $18B+ of freshly deployed capital. **The marginal advantage is
> measured in delivery windows, not knowledge.**

## 5. The verdict

| | **Spec-locked equipment** | **Jobsite consumables** |
|---|---|---|
| Turns | <1× on made-to-order | 2.7–4.8× |
| Gross margin | ~22% (electrical dist.) | 39–45% |
| **Where money is made** | **Getting specified; hitting the date** | **Density, logistics, working capital** |
| Working capital | Deposits, low stock | Heavy inventory, many SKUs |
| Barrier | **Franchise appointment — closed** | **Capital and branch density — open but expensive** |
| Customer | Estimator/PM at buyout | Foreman at 6 a.m. |
| Threat | Incumbent who holds the line | Home Depot/SRS and White Cap, both rolling up |

**Your spec-reading edge is worth exactly zero in consumables.** Nobody
specifies a glove. That business is won with a truck, a branch, a credit line
and a 6 a.m. delivery window — dimensions where your skill is irrelevant and
Home Depot's $18.25B is decisive.

**Your spec-reading edge is the entire value on the other side.** The evidence
above shows the constraint usually is not the factory: Republic ships frames in
6–7 weeks at 98% on-time; Allegion ships most mechanical hardware in 2–5 weeks.
**But shop drawings take 9–10 weeks, curtainwall approval takes 60–90 days, and
a mag lock takes 16–20 weeks inside a hardware set where everything else takes
three.** Every one of those is a *reading* failure, caught only by someone who
opens the schedule and the spec section before buyout.

**But the highest-value spec-locked categories are structurally closed.** So:

> ### Attack Division 8 and Division 10 — openings and specialties.
>
> - **Lines are obtainable.** Hollow metal, hardware, and Bobrick/Bradley/ASI/
>   Scranton distribution are all fragmented and regional.
> - **The margin is in labor, not inventory** — hardware scheduling, keying
>   schedules, submittal packages, frame elevations, Div 28 coordination. That
>   is a low-working-capital, service-margin profile, not a 22% commodity one.
> - **Geography is on your side.** Allegion's own shipping table puts Republic's
>   plant in **Tennessee, 1–2 day transit to CST and 2–3 day to EST** — the
>   shortest transit in Allegion's network for this corridor.
> - **The failure mode is documented, current, and almost nobody reads it.** The
>   16–20 week mag lock. The 12-week special. The +5-week Buy America adder. The
>   20-week finish. A supplier who flags these *at bid time, before the GC
>   commits a date* is selling schedule certainty — the thing actually scarce.
> - **Openings are where Divisions 8, 9, 10, 26 and 28 collide**, and access
>   control is the fastest-growing, most mis-coordinated part of it.

### Three ways this is wrong — hold these

1. **Division 8 is a contracting business as much as a supply business.** The
   margin lives in hardware scheduling and submittal labor, which means hiring
   or becoming a DHI-credentialed hardware consultant (AHC). **That is a hiring
   problem, not a purchasing problem, and it is the real barrier.**
2. **The regional market is down 16.3%.** Doors follow starts. This is a bad
   two years to start one in this corridor — which argues for building the
   capability now and being ready, not for signing a lease.
3. **I could not verify Div 8/10 distributor gross margins from any public
   source.** The "higher margin than electrical" claim is inference from channel
   structure — fragmented, service-intensive, no national price transparency —
   **not data. Validate with actual quotes before committing capital.**

## 6. How this connects to the rest of the file

`06-the-stronger-play.md` said the defensible business is the value-added
package, not the sourcing arbitrage. `10-the-channel-problem.md` said CFS is a
franchised channel you have to be appointed into. **This report says the same
thing a third time from a third direction:** every category where the gap
between "specified" and "available" is largest is a category you cannot enter,
and the one you *can* enter pays for reading, coordination and date certainty —
not for holding inventory.

**Three independent research threads have now landed on the same conclusion.
The product is competence delivered before buyout. Everything else is a truck.**
