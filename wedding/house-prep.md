# House Prep — Wedding Party Staying Over

Hosting the wedding party at your house is two jobs, not one: running a small B&B for a few nights, and (if anyone gets ready there on wedding morning) turning one room into a staging area that will be photographed. Both are easy if you decide a few things early and work the list below.

## Decide these first — everything else follows

1. **Exactly who is staying, and which nights?** Get a real list with a headcount per night. "The wedding party" can mean 4 people or 12 once partners are counted.
2. **Is anyone getting ready at the house on wedding morning?** Hair/makeup at the house changes the whole plan (see the staging section below). If HMU happens at the venue or a hotel, your job is just beds, bathrooms, and breakfast.
3. **Where do YOU TWO sleep on the wedding night?** Decide now. Many couples book a hotel for the wedding night even when the house is full — coming home at midnight to a houseful of guests is not the vibe. Either answer is fine; deciding late is not.
4. **Kids or pets in the mix?** Theirs or yours — plan where each goes during the chaos (a pet sitter on wedding day is cheap insurance).

## Sleeping assignments

Make an actual room chart — assigning rooms on arrival night in the driveway is how feelings get hurt.

| Room / space | Sleeps | Who | Bed type | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guest room | 2 | | Queen | Real beds go to elders / couples first |
| Office | 1–2 | | Air mattress | |
| Living room | 1–2 | | Sofa bed / air mattress | Last to bed, first woken — assign to the easygoing one |
| | | | | |

**Linen math per person:** 1 set of sheets + 1 pillow + 1 blanket + 2 towels + 1 washcloth. Count what you own, borrow or buy the gap now, not the week of. Air mattresses: test-inflate every one **this week** — the one that's been in the closet for three years has a leak.

## Bathrooms — the real bottleneck

- Do the math: one shower serving 6 people on wedding morning at ~20 minutes each is a 2-hour queue. If HMU starts early, post a **shower schedule** the night before (earliest HMU slot showers first). It feels bossy; everyone will thank you.
- Stock visibly: double the toilet paper you think you need, a plunger in every bathroom (visible, so nobody has to ask), extra towels, basic toiletries people forget (toothpaste, razors, ibuprofen), a nightlight, counter space cleared of your own stuff.
- Run the water heater's limits honestly — if it can't do 4 showers back-to-back, the schedule matters even more.

## Kitchen & food

- **Self-serve breakfast station**: coffee (borrow a second maker or get a big thermal carafe — one 10-cup pot does not serve 8 people), mugs out, bagels/fruit/yogurt/granola bars. Nobody should have to ask where anything is.
- **Wedding morning is not a cooking morning.** Pre-order or prep the night before; protein matters more than pastry (see the playbook: you eat breakfast on wedding day, non-negotiable).
- Label a fridge zone for guest food; clear a shelf.
- Water bottles everywhere. A case in every common room disappears faster than you think.
- Night-before dinner: decide it now (pizza order, taco bar, rehearsal-dinner leftovers) so it's a plan, not a 9pm negotiation.

## Two-weeks-out punch list (the PM pass)

Walk your own house like a superintendent doing a pre-punch:

- [ ] Fix the stuff you've stopped noticing: squeaky doors, the sticky lock, the toilet that runs, burned-out bulbs
- [ ] Test smoke/CO detectors (a 3am low-battery chirp with a full house is a memory nobody wants)
- [ ] Wifi: put the network + password on a card in each guest room
- [ ] Keys/entry: spare keys or a door code shared with the party; decide if the door stays unlocked wedding morning for vendors
- [ ] Clear closet + drawer space in each guest room (6 hanging spots per person minimum — there will be garment bags)
- [ ] Book a cleaning service for 3–4 days out (and ideally the week after) — this is the single best money spent in this whole file
- [ ] Extra trash bags, and know your pickup day; a full house makes triple the garbage
- [ ] Parking plan: who parks where, where the shuttle/cars stage on wedding morning, neighbor heads-up if the street fills up
- [ ] Phone-charger basket in the common area (multi-port charger + cables)
- [ ] First-aid kit + the emergency kit staged (see the playbook's handoff package)

## Protect the house (and your sanity)

- **The stash room:** pick one room or closet, declare it off-limits, and move all your clutter, valuables, medications, important papers, and anything breakable-and-precious into it. Lock it if you can. This is the trick that makes a "clean whole house" achievable in one weekend.
- Put away anything you'd be sad about: the good rug under the red-wine zone, the heirloom on the shelf at elbow height.
- Rings, gifts, cards, marriage license: one designated lockable spot, one designated owner.
- Quiet-hours expectation set kindly in advance ("we're up at 6 on Saturday, so we're calling it at 11 on Friday") — it lands better as a heads-up than as a 11:30pm request.

## Wedding-morning staging (only if getting ready at the house)

This room will be in your photos and video. Choose and prep it like a set:

- **Pick the room with the best natural light** — big windows, ideally not facing away from the morning sun. HMU artists will ask for exactly this.
- Tall chairs/stools (HMU artists work standing), a table for kits, **power strips** — dryers, irons, and wands trip more breakers than any other residential load you'll host this year.
- Clear the floor and surfaces completely: the photographer's detail shots (dress hanging, shoes, invitations, rings) happen here, and background clutter is forever.
- A sturdy hook or pretty hanger spot for the dress/suits (a windowed door or wardrobe front, not the shower rod).
- Steamer and lint roller staged; a bedsheet on the floor if the dress goes on in a carpeted room.
- Dress-code the morning: robes or button-downs/matching shirts — nothing that pulls over the head after hair is done.
- **No red drinks near the attire.** Mimosas earn their reputation for a reason; white wine, water, and coffee with lids only in the staging room.
- Speaker + playlist, snacks that don't stain or smear (fruit, croissants — no powdered sugar, no hot sauce).
- Lunch delivered before anyone leaves — this is on the day-of run sheet already; the house host just needs to answer the door.

## Front-room conversion: closing it off on a budget

Turning the open front room into a private guest room means a door across the opening. Cheapest paths, in order:

1. **Used French doors — Habitat for Humanity ReStore first.** Salvage doors commonly run **$50–150/leaf** there vs. $349+ online; inventory turns over weekly, so check in person and often. Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist ("french doors", "15-lite door") are the other free-to-cheap sources — people practically give these away after remodels.
2. **New hollow-core bifolds** if salvage strikes out: big-box bifolds run roughly **$60–100 per 24–36" panel** at the low end (louver/6-panel hollow core), ~$170–240 for wider composite sets — hardware usually included. No glass, but cheap, light, and easy to hang on a track.
3. Wildcard: a **double curtain rod + heavy curtains** ($40–80 total) is the zero-carpentry fallback that makes the room private by the wedding even if the door project slips.

**Estimator's checklist before buying anything used:**
- Measure the finished opening (width × height × jamb depth) and carry the numbers with you — salvage is cheap *because* the sizes are odd. A pair of 30" leaves needs a ~60¼" opening; trimming a solid wood door is fine, trimming hollow-core is not (you'll hit the void).
- French door pairs: confirm both leaves match, hinges included, and check for rot at the bottom rail and rattling/cracked lites. Missing hardware is a $20 fix; a twisted slab is a wall decoration.
- Bifolds hang from a **track in the head jamb** — verify there's solid framing above the opening to screw into, and that the opening height is a true 80"+ for standard panels.
- Old painted doors (pre-1978 house stock): assume lead paint — don't sand, just clean and topcoat, or skip.

## Countdown

| When | Do |
|---|---|
| 2 weeks out | Punch-list walk, linen/mattress inventory + test-inflate, room chart drafted, cleaner booked, night-before dinner decided |
| 1 week out | Groceries ordered for delivery day-before, wifi cards printed, stash room loaded, parking plan shared with the party |
| 2 days out | Cleaner comes, beds made, bathroom stocking done, breakfast station assembled |
| Night before | Shower schedule posted, coffee prepped on a timer, chargers out, house at 11pm quiet |
| Morning of | You make coffee, eat breakfast, and let the house run off this list instead of off you |

One last thing from the playbook that applies double here: hosting the party means your house is not your quiet retreat the week of the wedding. Batch the prep into one weekend with this list, delegate the morning-of hosting to a wedding-party member ("you own the coffee and the door"), and remember the guests staying with you are your favorite people — a slightly imperfect house full of them is the point.
