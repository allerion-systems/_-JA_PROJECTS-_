# Metal stud import tariffs — China vs. the world

*Sept 5, 2026. Prompted by a Chinese roll-former's catalog (metric drywall studs/track,
90×40 / 160×60mm, 0.3–1.2mm). Companion artifact: "Stud Import Tariff Brief".*

## Verdict
China is the WORST origin right now (~85% duty stack). Cheaper origins exist —
Mexico first, Vietnam second (~50–60%) — but the Apr 2026 Section 232 rewrite
(50% on FULL invoice value of mostly-steel goods, every country) flattened the
arbitrage: importing saves ~$0.70–1.50/stick over U.S. wholesale at container
risk. And the cataloged product is metric — U.S. drywall studs are ASTM C645
imperial profiles; metric studs are commercially unsellable here without custom
tooling.

## The stack (rates are law as of this date; they move)
| Layer | Rate | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Section 232 steel | 50% of full customs value (since Apr 6, 2026) | every country |
| Section 301 | 25% | China only (List 3 covers structural steel) |
| Section 122 | 10% | all countries; replaced IEEPA tariffs after SCOTUS struck them Feb 20, 2026; statutorily capped 150 days without Congress — unstable |
| AD/CVD | 0–200%+ | none on finished studs from China today; orders exist on Chinese corrosion-resistant flat steel (the input coil) — scope-creep risk, broker screen required |

Notes: 232 goods are excluded from the old reciprocal tariffs; 301 and the
122 layer stack ON TOP of 232. Vietnam's CPTPP membership does nothing for US
imports (US not a member). USMCA does NOT exempt Canada/Mexico from the 50%.

## Plays
1. **Now:** dealer accounts with SCAFCO / ClarkDietrich / Telling distributors;
   retail near big-box $7.49 with wholesale at $3.50–5 — margin without import risk.
2. **If importing:** Mexico first — imperial tooling exists, truck freight, no
   China penalty. Three quotes before any Asia order.
3. **Long term:** the 50% tariff protects whoever FORMS the stud. US-melted coil
   crosses no tariff wall; a roll-forming line runs ~$80–250K. Be the mill.

## Red lines (standing)
DDP "tariffs included" offers with second invoices, friendly HTS codes, or
Malaysia routing = undervaluation / misclassification / transshipment = customs
fraud, penalties per-entry and personal. Walk. Before any PO: broker-confirmed
HTS + rates in writing, AD/CVD scope screen, mill certs (ASTM A653, C645,
AISI S100).
